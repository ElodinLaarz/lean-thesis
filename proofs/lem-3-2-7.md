# Lemma 3.2.7: minimal p-power in a principal local ideal

## Statement

**Notation and standing hypotheses.** Throughout, $d < 0$ is an integer with $d \equiv 0$ or
$1 \pmod 4$ (a *discriminant*). Write $d = D f^2$, where $D$ is the discriminant of the
imaginary quadratic field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ (the *fundamental discriminant*) and
$f \geq 1$ is the *conductor* of $d$. Set

$$\tau := \frac{d + \sqrt{d}}{2}, \qquad \mathcal{O}_d := \mathbb{Z}[\tau] = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}\tau,$$

the order of discriminant $d$ in $K$. The minimal polynomial of $\tau$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is

$$g(x) = x^2 - dx + \frac{d^2 - d}{4} \in \mathbb{Z}[x],$$

so $\mathcal{O}_d \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(g(x))$. Let $\alpha \mapsto \bar{\alpha}$ denote the
nontrivial automorphism of $K/\mathbb{Q}$; thus $\bar{\tau} = d - \tau$ (Vieta:
$\tau + \bar{\tau} = d$, $\tau\bar{\tau} = \tfrac{d^2-d}{4}$), and the field norm is
$N(\alpha) = \alpha\bar{\alpha}$. For $a \in \mathbb{Z}$,

$$N(\tau - a) = (\tau - a)(\bar{\tau} - a) = a^2 - da + \tfrac{d^2 - d}{4} = g(a),$$

and since $d < 0$ we have $g(a) = \left(a - \tfrac{d}{2}\right)^2 - \tfrac{d}{4} > 0$; in
particular $g(a)$ is a *positive* rational integer, so its $p\text{-adic}$ valuation is finite.
We write $v_p$ for the $p\text{-adic}$ valuation on $\mathbb{Q}^\times$ (normalized by
$v_p(p) = 1$) and $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \{x \in \mathbb{Q} : v_p(x) \geq 0\}$ for the localization
of $\mathbb{Z}$ at $(p)$.

We assume the standing hypotheses of thesis §3.2, as in the document [lem-3-2-2]: $p$ is a
rational prime **dividing the conductor** $f$ (and $w := v_p(f) \geq 1$, though $w$ plays no
role in this lemma). Under $p \mid f$, the reduction $g \bmod p$ is the square of a linear
polynomial, so by Proposition 3.2.1 there is a **unique** prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of
$\mathcal{O}_d$ containing $p$; in particular $p$ is non-split in $\mathcal{O}_d$.
(Justification: $v_p(d) = v_p(D) + 2w \geq 2$. For $p$ odd this gives $g \equiv x^2 \pmod p$.
For $p = 2$: if $v_2(d) \geq 3$ then $g \equiv x^2 \pmod 2$, while if $v_2(d) = 2$ then
$\tfrac{d^2-d}{4} = \tfrac{d}{4}(d-1)$ is odd and $g \equiv x^2 + 1 = (x+1)^2 \pmod 2$.) Let
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} := \{\alpha/\sigma : \alpha \in \mathcal{O}_d,\ \sigma \in \mathcal{O}_d \setminus \mathfrak{p}\} \subseteq K$
be the localization at $\mathfrak{p}$; since
$\mathcal{O}_d \subseteq \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq K$, the contraction to
$\mathcal{O}_d$ of an ideal of $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is its intersection with
$\mathcal{O}_d$ inside $K$. By Lemma 3.2.2 [lem-3-2-2], since $p$ is non-split,

$$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau].$$

Finally, for integers $k, m$ with $m/2 \leq k \leq m$ and $A \in \mathbb{Z}$ with
$g(A) \equiv 0 \pmod{p^{2k-m}}$, the *canonical ideal* $(p^k,\, p^{m-k}(\tau - A))$ denotes the
ideal of $\mathcal{O}_d$ generated by $p^k$ and $p^{m-k}(\tau - A)$, as in Lemma 3.2.4
[lem-3-2-4].

> **Lemma 3.2.7.** Keep the standing hypotheses above (in particular $p \mid f$). Let $r \geq 0$
> be an integer and $a \in \mathbb{Z}$, and set
> $$\nu := v_p\!\left(N(\tau - a)\right) = v_p(g(a)) \geq 0.$$
> Then the contraction to $\mathcal{O}_d$ of the principal ideal
> $p^r(\tau - a)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is the canonical ideal with parameters
> $k = r + \nu$, $m = 2r + \nu$, $A = a$:
> $$p^r(\tau - a)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \cap \mathcal{O}_d \;=\; \bigl(p^{r+\nu},\; p^r(\tau - a)\bigr).$$
> In particular, every nonzero rational number lying in
> $p^r(\tau - a)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ has $p\text{-adic}$ valuation at least $r + \nu$,
> and the smallest positive rational integer — equivalently, the smallest power of $p$ —
> contained in $p^r(\tau - a)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is exactly $p^{r+\nu}$.

Note that the parameters are legitimate for Lemma 3.2.4:
$m/2 = r + \nu/2 \leq r + \nu = k \leq 2r + \nu = m$, and the admissibility condition
$g(a) \equiv 0 \pmod{p^{2k - m}} = p^{\nu}$ holds by the definition of $\nu$ (indeed with exact
valuation).

## Role in the development

This is the local half of the classification of locally principal $\mathfrak{p}\text{-primary}$
ideals. Lemma 3.2.8 decides equality of two principal local ideals by comparing their
contractions, which this lemma identifies as canonical ideals of Lemma 3.2.4; the uniqueness of
canonical parameters then yields the equality criterion. In §3.3 these two lemmas together
convert the count of invertible (equivalently, locally principal) ideals of index $p^m$ in
Corollary 3.1.3 into a count of residues $\alpha$ with $v_p(g(\alpha)) = 2k - m$, which is then
evaluated by the root-counting Lemma 3.2.6. The proof below uses Lemma 3.2.2 (structure of
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$) and the canonical-ideal formalism of Lemma 3.2.4.

## Proof

Throughout, fix $r \geq 0$, $a \in \mathbb{Z}$, and write

$$g(a) = p^{\nu} u, \qquad u \in \mathbb{Z}_{> 0},\quad p \nmid u,$$

which is possible since $g(a)$ is a positive integer; note $u \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^\times$.

**Step 1 (quadratic relation for $\tau - a$).** The elements $\tau - a$ and $\bar{\tau} - a$
have sum $(\tau + \bar{\tau}) - 2a = d - 2a$ and product $N(\tau - a) = g(a)$, so $\tau - a$
satisfies

$$(\tau - a)^2 = (d - 2a)(\tau - a) - g(a). \tag{1}$$

**Step 2 ($\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is free of rank $2$ over $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$).** By Lemma
3.2.2 and the standing hypothesis $p \mid f$ (which makes $p$ non-split, as recalled above),
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$. Since
$\tau^2 = d\tau - \tfrac{d^2-d}{4} \in \mathbb{Z}\tau + \mathbb{Z}$, we get
$\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau] = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} + \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\tau$, and the sum is direct
because $1, \tau$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$ ($\tau \notin \mathbb{Q}$ as
$d < 0$). Thus every $\beta \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is uniquely $\beta = e_0 + e_1\tau$
with $e_0, e_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$; likewise
$\mathcal{O}_d = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}\tau$, and every element of $K$ has unique
coordinates in the $\mathbb{Q}\text{-basis}$ $(1, \tau)$.

**Step 3 (normal form of the local principal ideal).** We claim

$$p^r(\tau - a)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \;=\; \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\, p^{r+\nu} \;+\; \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\, p^r(\tau - a). \tag{2}$$

Let $\beta = e_0 + e_1 \tau \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ and write
$\beta = (e_0 + a e_1) + e_1(\tau - a)$. Using (1),

$$(\tau - a)\beta = (e_0 + a e_1)(\tau - a) + e_1 (\tau - a)^2 = \bigl[e_0 + (d - a) e_1\bigr](\tau - a) - e_1\, g(a),$$

and multiplying by $p^r$ and substituting $g(a) = p^{\nu}u$:

$$p^r(\tau - a)\,\beta \;=\; \lambda \cdot p^r(\tau - a) \;+\; \mu \cdot p^{r + \nu}, \qquad \lambda := e_0 + (d - a)e_1, \quad \mu := -u\, e_1. \tag{3}$$

The $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\text{-linear}$ map $(e_0, e_1) \mapsto (\lambda, \mu)$ has matrix
$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & d - a \\ 0 & -u \end{pmatrix}$ of determinant
$-u \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^{\times}$, hence is a bijection of $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^2$ onto itself
(inverse: $e_1 = -u^{-1}\mu$, $e_0 = \lambda + (d-a)u^{-1}\mu$). Since the principal ideal is
exactly $\{p^r(\tau - a)\beta : \beta \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}\}$, formula (3) with
$(\lambda, \mu)$ ranging over all of $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^2$ gives (2).

**Step 4 (containment $\supseteq$).** Both generators of the canonical ideal lie in the
contraction. Indeed $p^r(\tau - a) \in \mathcal{O}_d$ and, trivially,
$p^r(\tau - a) \in p^r(\tau - a)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$. For the integer generator:
$\bar{\tau} - a = (d - a) - \tau \in \mathcal{O}_d$ and

$$p^r(\tau - a)(\bar{\tau} - a) = p^r g(a) = p^{r+\nu} u,$$

so
$p^{r+\nu} = p^r(\tau - a) \cdot u^{-1}(\bar{\tau} - a) \in p^r(\tau - a)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$,
using $u^{-1} \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$; and of course
$p^{r+\nu} \in \mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d$. The contraction is an ideal of
$\mathcal{O}_d$ containing both generators, hence contains the ideal
$\bigl(p^{r+\nu}, p^r(\tau - a)\bigr)$ they generate.

**Step 5 (containment $\subseteq$).** Let
$\xi \in p^r(\tau - a)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \cap \mathcal{O}_d$. By (2),
$\xi = \mu\, p^{r+\nu} + \lambda\, p^r(\tau - a)$ for some $\lambda, \mu \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$.
In the $\mathbb{Q}\text{-basis}$ $(1, \tau)$ of $K$,

$$\xi = \bigl(\mu\, p^{r+\nu} - a \lambda\, p^r\bigr)\cdot 1 + \bigl(\lambda\, p^r\bigr)\cdot \tau.$$

Since $\xi \in \mathcal{O}_d = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}\tau$ and coordinates in the basis
$(1,\tau)$ are unique, both coordinates are rational integers:

$$\lambda\, p^r \in \mathbb{Z}, \qquad \mu\, p^{r+\nu} - a\lambda\, p^r \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

From $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ we get $v_p(\lambda) \geq 0$; from
$\lambda p^r \in \mathbb{Z}$ we get $v_q(\lambda) = v_q(\lambda p^r) \geq 0$ for every prime
$q \neq p$. Hence $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}$. Consequently $a\lambda p^r \in \mathbb{Z}$, so
$\mu\, p^{r+\nu} \in \mathbb{Z}$, and the same two-valuation argument gives
$\mu \in \mathbb{Z}$. Therefore

$$\xi = \mu \cdot p^{r+\nu} + \lambda \cdot p^r(\tau - a), \qquad \lambda, \mu \in \mathbb{Z},$$

is in particular an $\mathcal{O}_d\text{-linear}$ combination of the two generators, i.e.
$\xi \in \bigl(p^{r+\nu}, p^r(\tau - a)\bigr)$.

Steps 4 and 5 prove the contraction identity. (Note that Step 5 in fact lands in the
$\mathbb{Z}\text{-span}$ $\mathbb{Z}p^{r+\nu} + \mathbb{Z}p^r(\tau - a)$; combined with Step 4
this shows the chain
$\mathbb{Z}\text{-span} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d\text{-ideal} \subseteq \text{contraction} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}\text{-span}$
collapses, recovering for these parameters the "$\mathbb{Z}\text{-spans}$ suffice" principle of
Remark 3.2.5, part of [lem-3-2-4].)

**Step 6 (minimality of the $p\text{-power}$).** Let
$\rho \in p^r(\tau - a)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \cap \mathbb{Q}$ be nonzero (this covers every
rational integer in the local ideal). By (2), $\rho = \mu\, p^{r+\nu} + \lambda\, p^r(\tau - a)$
with $\lambda, \mu \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$; the $\tau\text{-coordinate}$ of $\rho$ is
$\lambda p^r$ and must vanish, so $\lambda = 0$ and $\rho = \mu\, p^{r+\nu}$, whence

$$v_p(\rho) = v_p(\mu) + r + \nu \geq r + \nu.$$

Conversely $p^{r+\nu}$ lies in the ideal by Step 4. Hence the rational integers contained in
$p^r(\tau - a)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ are exactly those $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ with
$v_p(n) \geq r + \nu$ (for such $n$, $n = (n/p^{r+\nu})\cdot p^{r+\nu}$ with
$n/p^{r+\nu} \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$), and the smallest positive one is $p^{r+\nu}$.

$\blacksquare$

## Remarks

**Remark 1 (Divergence from the thesis: variable renamed $s \to a$).** The thesis states the
lemma as: "For any integers $r$ and $s$ where $r \geq 0$, the ideal
$(p^r(\tau - s))\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ contains minimal, positive integer
$p^{r + v_p(N(\tau - s))}$." We rename the shift variable $s$ to $a$, following ERRATA E6.7:
Lemma 3.2.6 uses $s$ for the modulus exponent in $x^2 \equiv p^{2r}u \pmod{p^s}$, and §3.3
applies both lemmas in the same computation, so keeping both uses of $s$ would be a genuine
collision. The letter $a$ also matches its role: the contraction is the canonical ideal of Lemma
3.2.4 with residue parameter $A = a \bmod p^{\nu}$.

**Remark 2 (Divergence from the thesis: standing hypotheses made explicit; the statement is
false for split $p$).** The thesis's Lemma 3.2.7 carries no hypotheses beyond $r \geq 0$,
$s \in \mathbb{Z}$; it silently relies on the §3.2 standing hypothesis $p \mid f$ (declared
several paragraphs earlier), which forces $p$ non-split and makes the notation
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ refer to the unique prime above $p$. Per ERRATA E6.2 the hypothesis must be restated: **the
lemma is false for split $p$.** Counterexample: $d = -4$ (so $f = 1$),
$\mathcal{O}_d = \mathbb{Z}[i]$ with $\tau = -2 + i$ and $g(x) = x^2 + 4x + 5$. The prime
$p = 5$ splits: $g \equiv x(x - 1) \pmod 5$, giving two primes $\mathfrak{p} = (5, \tau)$ and
$\mathfrak{p}' = (5, \tau - 1)$. Take $r = 0$, $a = 0$, so $\nu = v_5(N(\tau)) = v_5(5) = 1$.
Since $\tau \notin \mathfrak{p}'$, the element $\tau$ is a unit of
$\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}'}$, so
$\tau\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}'} = \mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}'}$: its contraction is
$\mathcal{O}_d$, not $(5, \tau) = \mathfrak{p}$, and the minimal positive integer in it is
$1 = 5^0 \neq 5^{r + \nu}$. (For split $p$ even the phrase *the prime above* $p$ in the
statement is ill-defined; and $\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}'} \supsetneq \mathbb{Z}_{(5)}[\tau]$,
so Step 2 fails.) The proof shows what is really needed:
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$, i.e. the equality case of Lemma 3.2.2,
which holds for **every non-split** $p$; the lemma is therefore true in that wider generality,
but the formal statement follows §3.2 and carries $p \mid f$.

**Remark 3 (Divergence from the thesis: statement strengthened to the contraction identity).**
The thesis asserts only the minimal-positive-integer claim. However, the thesis's own proof of
Lemma 3.2.8 uses more, citing 3.2.7 for it: "the intersections with $\mathcal{O}$ are the
$\mathfrak{p}\text{-primary}$ ideals $(p^{r + v_p(N(\tau - \alpha))}, p^r(\tau - \alpha))$."
That contraction identity is what we take as the main statement (and what the intended Lean
declaration expresses); the minimal-integer assertion is the "in particular." Our proof delivers
both simultaneously via the normal form (2).

**Remark 4 (Relation to the thesis's proof).** The thesis proof works in the basis
$(1, \sqrt{d}/2)$: it notes
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau] = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\sqrt{d}/2]$ (valid since
$\sqrt{d}/2 = \tau - d/2$ and $d/2 \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$: for odd $p$ because $2$ is invertible
in $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, for $p = 2$ because $p \mid f$ forces $4 \mid d$), rewrites
$p^r(\tau - a) = p^r(\sqrt{d}/2 + c)$ with $c := d/2 - a \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ (the thesis calls
this constant $a$ — another collision our renaming avoids), and argues: if
$p^r(\sqrt{d}/2 + c)\beta$ is rational for $\beta = b_0 + b_1\sqrt{d}/2$, the coefficient of
$\sqrt{d}/2$ vanishes, i.e. $\det\begin{pmatrix} c & 1 \\ b_0 & -b_1 \end{pmatrix} = 0$; this
rank-$1$ condition makes $(b_0, -b_1)$ proportional to $(c, 1)$, so
$\beta = \lambda(\bar{\tau} - a)$ with $\lambda = -b_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ — "the conjugate of
$\beta$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\text{-multiple}$ of $\tau - a$," and then
$p^r(\tau - a)\beta = \lambda\, p^r g(a)$ has $v_p \geq r + \nu$ (this last valuation
computation is left implicit in the thesis). Our Step 6 is exactly this argument transposed to
the basis $(1, \tau)$: the vanishing of the $\tau\text{-coordinate}$ forces $\lambda = 0$ in
(2), i.e. $\rho \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}p^{r+\nu}$. Working with $(1, \tau)$ avoids the $d/2$ parity
discussion entirely, and packaging the whole ideal as the normal form (2) is what upgrades the
minimality argument to the contraction identity of Remark 3.

**Remark 5 (Exactness of $\nu$, index, and how the lemma is consumed).** The definition
$\nu = v_p(g(a))$ is the *exact* valuation: the unit-ness of $u = g(a)/p^{\nu}$ in
$\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ is used in Step 3 (invertibility of the change of coordinates) and Step 4
($u^{-1} \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$); with a strict underestimate of $\nu$ both the identity and the
minimality claim fail. From the proof, the contraction is the free $\mathbb{Z}\text{-module}$
with basis $\{p^{r+\nu},\, p^r(\tau - a)\}$; its index in
$\mathcal{O}_d = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}\tau$ is the determinant
$\left|\det\begin{pmatrix} p^{r+\nu} & -a p^r \\ 0 & p^r \end{pmatrix}\right| = p^{2r + \nu} = p^{v_p(N(p^r(\tau - a)))}$,
consistent with Lemma 3.2.4's converse ($m = 2r + \nu$). In §3.3 the lemma is applied with
$k := r + \nu$ and $m := 2r + \nu$ fixed: a locally principal $\mathfrak{p}\text{-primary}$
ideal of index $p^m$ whose minimal integer is $p^k$ is the contraction of
$p^{m-k}(\tau - \alpha)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ for some $\alpha$ with
$v_p(g(\alpha)) = 2k - m$ exactly — "a root of $g$ mod $p^{2k-m}$ but not mod $p^{2k-m+1}$,"
which is the count Lemma 3.2.6 then evaluates.
