# Lemma 3.2.6 (corrected/completed) — solutions of $x^2 \equiv p^{2r}u \pmod{p^s}$

## Statement

Throughout, $p$ is a rational prime and $v_p$ denotes the $p\text{-adic}$ valuation on $\mathbb{Z}$ (with $v_p(0) = \infty$). For an odd prime $p$ and $\gcd(u,p)=1$, $\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)$ is the Legendre symbol. For an integer $n \geq 1$ we write $\mathbb{Z}/n$ for the ring of residues modulo $n$; an equation $x^2 = c$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p^s$ is the same as the congruence $x^2 \equiv c \pmod{p^s}$ for integer representatives. For $c \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s$ set
$$N_s(c) \;:=\; \#\{\,x \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s : x^2 = c\,\}.$$

> **Lemma 3.2.6 (corrected/completed).** Let $p$ be a prime, $s \geq 1$ an integer, $r \geq 0$ an integer, and $u \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $\gcd(u, p) = 1$.
>
> **(i) (Even valuation.)** Assume $2r < s$.
>
> * If $p$ is **odd**, then
>   $$N_s\big(p^{2r}u\big) \;=\; \begin{cases} 2p^r & \text{if } \left(\frac{u}{p}\right) = 1,\\[2pt] 0 & \text{if } \left(\frac{u}{p}\right) = -1. \end{cases}$$
>   Here the condition $\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)=1$ — i.e. $u$ is a quadratic residue **modulo** $p$ — is equivalent to $u$ being a quadratic residue modulo $p^{s-2r}$ (and modulo any positive power of $p$); see Step 2(b) of the proof.
>
> * If $p = 2$, then the count is governed by the difference $s - 2r$, **not** by $s$:
>   $$N_s\big(2^{2r}u\big) \;=\; \begin{cases} 2^{r} & \text{if } s-2r = 1 \text{ (no condition on } u\text{)},\\[2pt] 2^{r+1} & \text{if } s-2r = 2 \text{ and } u \equiv 1 \pmod{4},\\[2pt] 2^{r+2} & \text{if } s-2r \geq 3 \text{ and } u \equiv 1 \pmod{8},\\[2pt] 0 & \text{if } s-2r = 2,\ u \equiv 3 \pmod 4,\ \text{ or } s-2r \geq 3,\ u \not\equiv 1 \pmod 8. \end{cases}$$
>
> **(ii) (Odd valuation.)** If $2r + 1 < s$, then $N_s\big(p^{2r+1}u\big) = 0$: the congruence $x^2 \equiv p^{2r+1}u \pmod{p^s}$ has no solutions. (This holds for **every** prime $p$, including $p = 2$.)
>
> **(iii) (Zero.)** $N_s(0) = p^{\lfloor s/2 \rfloor}$.

**Exhaustiveness.** Every $c \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s$ is covered by exactly one part. If $c \neq 0$, any two integer lifts of $c$ differ by a multiple of $p^s$ and have $p\text{-adic}$ valuation $v < s$; hence $v := v_p(c)$ is well defined, and $c = p^v u$ with $u$ a lift-unit. Part (i) applies when $v$ is even, part (ii) when $v$ is odd; the hypotheses $2r < s$, resp. $2r+1 < s$, are automatic since $v < s$. If $c = 0$, part (iii) applies. Thus the lemma computes $N_s(c)$ for **all** $c \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s$.

**Uniform packaging.** All cases of (i) are instances of the single reduction identity
$$N_s\big(p^{2r}u\big) \;=\; p^{r}\cdot N_{s-2r}(u) \qquad (2r < s,\ \gcd(u,p)=1), \tag{$*$}$$
combined with the unit-square-root counts $N_t(u)$ for $t := s - 2r \geq 1$:

| | $t = 1$ | $t = 2$ | $t \geq 3$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $p$ odd | $1 + \left(\frac{u}{p}\right)$ | $1 + \left(\frac{u}{p}\right)$ | $1 + \left(\frac{u}{p}\right)$ |
| $p = 2$ | $1$ | $2\cdot[u \equiv 1 \bmod 4]$ | $4\cdot[u \equiv 1 \bmod 8]$ |

## Role in the development

The ambient objects of the development (restated for self-containedness, though this lemma does not use them): $d < 0$ is an integer with $d \equiv 0, 1 \pmod 4$, $\mathcal{O}_d = \mathbb{Z}[\tau]$ with $\tau = \frac{d + \sqrt{d}}{2}$ is the imaginary quadratic order of discriminant $d$, $d = Df^2$ with $D$ the fundamental discriminant of $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ and $f \geq 1$ the conductor, $w = v_p(f)$, and $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}]$ is the index of an ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d$ as an additive subgroup. By the canonical-form Lemma 3.2.4, ideals $\mathfrak{a}$ with $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = p^m$ and minimal integer $p^k$ correspond bijectively to residues $A \bmod p^{2k-m}$ with $g(A) \equiv 0 \pmod{p^{2k-m}}$, where $g(x) = x^2 - dx + \frac{d^2 - d}{4}$ is the minimal polynomial of $\tau$; completing the square, $g(x) = (x - d/2)^2 - d/4$, turns this into the congruence $x^2 \equiv d/4 \pmod{p^{2k-m}}$ counted by the present lemma (with $s = 2k - m$, $p^{2r}u$ or $p^{2r+1}u$ or $0$ the class of $d/4$). It is therefore the sole "analytic input" to Theorem 3.1.2 and, downstream, Corollary 3.1.3. It uses nothing beyond elementary number theory and the structure of the unit groups $(\mathbb{Z}/p^t)^\times$, making it a candidate for Mathlib upstreaming.

## Proof

**Step 0 (well-definedness).** In (i) and (ii) the element $c = p^v u \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s$ (with $v = 2r$ or $2r+1$) is meant as the class of the integer $p^v u$; changing $u$ by a multiple of $p^{s-v}$ does not change $c$, and all statements depend on $u$ only through $u \bmod p^{s-2r}$ (part (i)) — indeed only through $u \bmod p$, $u \bmod 4$, or $u \bmod 8$ — consistently with the displayed conditions.

**Step 1 (stratification: proof of ($*$)).** Assume $2r < s$ and $\gcd(u,p) = 1$; put $t := s - 2r \geq 1$. Consider the two maps
$$\rho : \mathbb{Z}/p^{s-r} \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/p^{t}, \qquad \mu : \mathbb{Z}/p^{s-r} \to \mathbb{Z}/p^{s},$$
where $\rho$ is reduction (well defined since $t \leq s - r$, as $r \geq 0$) and $\mu(b) := p^r \tilde{b} \bmod p^s$ for any integer lift $\tilde{b}$ of $b$ ($\mu$ is well defined: replacing $\tilde{b}$ by $\tilde{b} + p^{s-r}c$ changes $p^r\tilde{b}$ by $p^s c$).

*Injectivity of* $\mu$: if $\mu(b) = \mu(b')$ then $p^s \mid p^r(\tilde{b} - \tilde{b}')$, so $p^{s-r} \mid \tilde{b} - \tilde{b}'$, i.e. $b = b'$.

*Every solution is in the image of* $\mu$: let $x \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s$ satisfy $x^2 = p^{2r}u$, and let $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ be a lift, so $p^s \mid a^2 - p^{2r}u$. Suppose $v_p(a) \leq r - 1$. Then $v_p(a^2) = 2v_p(a) \leq 2r - 2 < 2r = v_p(p^{2r}u)$, so the ultrametric equality for distinct valuations gives $v_p(a^2 - p^{2r}u) = 2v_p(a) < 2r < s$, contradicting $p^s \mid a^2 - p^{2r}u$. Hence $v_p(a) \geq r$; writing $a = p^r \tilde{b}$ we get $x = \mu(b)$ for $b := \tilde{b} \bmod p^{s-r}$.

*Solution criterion on the fiber coordinate*: for $b \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{s-r}$ with lift $\tilde{b}$,
$$\mu(b)^2 = p^{2r}u \ \text{ in } \mathbb{Z}/p^s \iff p^s \mid p^{2r}\big(\tilde{b}^2 - u\big) \iff p^{t} \mid \tilde{b}^2 - u \iff \rho(b)^2 = u \ \text{ in } \mathbb{Z}/p^{t}.$$
(The last equivalence is legitimate because $\tilde{b}^2 \bmod p^t$ depends only on $\rho(b)$.)

Therefore $\mu$ restricts to a bijection
$$\{\, b \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{s-r} : \rho(b)^2 = u \,\} \;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; \{\, x \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{s} : x^2 = p^{2r}u \,\}.$$
The left-hand set is $\rho^{-1}\big(\{y \in \mathbb{Z}/p^t : y^2 = u\}\big)$, and $\rho$ is a surjective homomorphism of cyclic groups of orders $p^{s-r}$ and $p^t$, so each fiber has exactly $p^{(s-r)-t} = p^{r}$ elements. Hence
$$N_s\big(p^{2r}u\big) = p^{r} \cdot N_{t}(u),$$
which is ($*$). $\square$ (Step 1)

**Step 2 (square roots of a unit modulo $p^t$).** Let $t \geq 1$ and $\gcd(u,p) = 1$; we compute $N_t(u)$.

First, any solution $y$ of $y^2 = u$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p^t$ is a unit: if $p$ divided a lift of $y$, then $p \mid u$, contradiction. So all solutions lie in the abelian group $G := (\mathbb{Z}/p^t)^\times$, of order $\varphi(p^t) = p^{t-1}(p-1)$. Let $\sigma : G \to G$, $\sigma(y) = y^2$, a group endomorphism. The fibers of $\sigma$ over its image are cosets of $\ker\sigma$; hence
$$N_t(u) = \begin{cases} |\ker \sigma| & \text{if } u \in \sigma(G),\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad\text{and}\qquad |\sigma(G)| = \frac{|G|}{|\ker\sigma|}. \tag{2.1}$$

**(a) Kernel, $p$ odd.** If $y^2 = 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p^t$ with integer lift $\tilde{y}$, then $p^t \mid (\tilde{y}-1)(\tilde{y}+1)$. Since $\gcd(\tilde{y}-1, \tilde{y}+1)$ divides $2$ and $p$ is odd, $p$ divides at most one of the two factors, so $p^t$ divides one of them: $y = \pm 1$. These are distinct because $p^t \geq 3$. Conversely $(\pm1)^2 = 1$. Thus $|\ker\sigma| = 2$.

**(b) Image, $p$ odd.** We claim $u \in \sigma(G) \iff \left(\frac{u}{p}\right) = 1$. Let $\pi : (\mathbb{Z}/p^t)^\times \to (\mathbb{Z}/p)^\times$ be the reduction map. It is surjective (an integer coprime to $p$ represents a unit at both levels), and $|\ker\pi| = \varphi(p^t)/\varphi(p) = p^{t-1}$, an **odd** number. Every element of a finite group of odd order $q$ is a square: $h = \big(h^{(q+1)/2}\big)^2$ since $h^q = 1$. Hence $\ker \pi \subseteq \sigma(G)$ — more precisely, every $h \in \ker\pi$ is a square in $\ker\pi \subseteq G$. Now:

* If $u = v^2 \in \sigma(G)$, then $\pi(u) = \pi(v)^2$ is a square in $(\mathbb{Z}/p)^\times$, so $\left(\frac{u}{p}\right) = 1$.
* Conversely, if $\pi(u) = \bar{w}^2$, choose $w \in G$ with $\pi(w) = \bar{w}$ (surjectivity). Then $\pi(u w^{-2}) = 1$, so $u w^{-2} \in \ker \pi$ is a square $v^2$, and $u = (wv)^2 \in \sigma(G)$.

Combining with (a) and (2.1): $N_t(u) = 2$ if $\left(\frac{u}{p}\right) = 1$ and $N_t(u) = 0$ otherwise; in particular, the condition that $u$ is a QR mod $p^t$ (for any $t \geq 1$) is equivalent to $u$ being a QR mod $p$, as claimed in the statement. (Alternatively: each of the two simple roots modulo $p$ — the derivative $2y$ is a unit — lifts uniquely through each modulus $p^j \to p^{j+1}$ by Hensel's lemma; this unique-lifting induction is the route the Lean proof takes.)

**(c) $p = 2$, $t = 1$.** $\mathbb{Z}/2 = \{0, 1\}$ and $u$ odd means $u = 1$; the equation $y^2 = y$ has the single unit solution $y = 1$. So $N_1(u) = 1$, unconditionally.

**(d) $p = 2$, $t = 2$.** $G = (\mathbb{Z}/4)^\times = \{1, 3\}$ and $1^2 = 3^2 = 1$. So $\sigma(G) = \{1\}$ and $|\ker\sigma| = 2$: $N_2(u) = 2$ if $u \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, else $0$.

**(e) Kernel, $p = 2$, $t \geq 3$.** Let $y^2 = 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2^t$, with odd integer lift $\tilde{y}$, so $2^t \mid (\tilde{y}-1)(\tilde{y}+1)$. The factors are consecutive even integers, so exactly one of them is $\equiv 2 \pmod 4$; the other must then have $2\text{-adic}$ valuation $\geq t - 1$. Hence $\tilde{y} \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{2^{t-1}}$, i.e. $y \in \{\pm 1,\ 2^{t-1} \pm 1\}$. Conversely, for $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}$ and $c \in \{0,1\}$,
$$\big(\varepsilon + c\,2^{t-1}\big)^2 = 1 + \varepsilon c\, 2^{t} + c^2 2^{2t-2} \equiv 1 \pmod{2^t}$$
since $2t - 2 \geq t$ for $t \geq 2$. The four elements $\pm 1, 2^{t-1}\pm 1$ are pairwise distinct modulo $2^t$ for $t \geq 3$ (their pairwise differences are $2,\ 2^{t-1},\ 2^{t-1}\pm 2$, none divisible by $2^t$). Thus $|\ker\sigma| = 4$.

**(f) Image, $p = 2$, $t \geq 3$.** Any odd integer $\tilde{y} = 2z + 1$ has $\tilde{y}^2 = 4z(z+1) + 1 \equiv 1 \pmod 8$ because $z(z+1)$ is even. Hence $\sigma(G) \subseteq H := \{ u \in G : u \equiv 1 \bmod 8 \}$. By (2.1) and (e), $|\sigma(G)| = 2^{t-1}/4 = 2^{t-3}$. On the other hand $|H| = 2^{t}/8 = 2^{t-3}$: the residues $\equiv 1 \pmod 8$ in $[0, 2^t)$ number $2^{t-3}$, and all are odd, hence units. Since $\sigma(G) \subseteq H$ and the cardinalities agree, $\sigma(G) = H$. With (2.1): $N_t(u) = 4$ if $u \equiv 1 \pmod 8$, else $0$. $\square$ (Step 2)

**Step 3 (proof of (i)).** Combine ($*$) with Step 2 at $t = s - 2r \geq 1$:

* $p$ odd: $N_s(p^{2r}u) = p^r \cdot \big(2 \text{ or } 0\big) = 2p^r$ if $\left(\frac{u}{p}\right) = 1$, else $0$.
* $p = 2$, $s - 2r = 1$: $N_s = 2^r \cdot 1 = 2^r$; the condition $u \equiv 1 \bmod 2$ is automatic for odd $u$, so no condition appears.
* $p = 2$, $s - 2r = 2$: $N_s = 2^r \cdot 2 = 2^{r+1}$ if $u \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, else $0$.
* $p = 2$, $s - 2r \geq 3$: $N_s = 2^r \cdot 4 = 2^{r+2}$ if $u \equiv 1 \pmod 8$, else $0$. $\square$ (i)

**Step 4 (proof of (ii)).** Assume $2r + 1 < s$ and suppose $x \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s$ satisfies $x^2 = p^{2r+1}u$; let $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ be a lift, so $p^s \mid a^2 - p^{2r+1}u$. Since $2r + 1 < s$, in particular $p^{2r+1} \mid a^2 - p^{2r+1}u$, hence $p^{2r+1} \mid a^2$, i.e. $2v_p(a) \geq 2r+1$; being even, $2v_p(a) \geq 2r + 2$. Then $v_p(a^2) \geq 2r+2 > 2r + 1 = v_p(p^{2r+1}u)$, and the ultrametric equality for distinct valuations gives
$$v_p\big(a^2 - p^{2r+1}u\big) = 2r+1 < s,$$
contradicting $p^s \mid a^2 - p^{2r+1}u$. (If $a = 0$ the same computation applies with $v_p(a^2) = \infty$.) So there is no solution. $\square$ (ii)

**Step 5 (proof of (iii)).** Set $\kappa := \lceil s/2 \rceil$, so $s - \kappa = \lfloor s/2 \rfloor$. We claim
$$\{\, x \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s : x^2 = 0 \,\} = p^{\kappa}\,\big(\mathbb{Z}/p^s\big),$$
the subgroup of multiples of $p^\kappa$.

($\supseteq$) If $x = p^{\kappa} y$ then $x^2 = p^{2\kappa} y^2 = 0$ since $2\kappa \geq s$.

($\subseteq$) If $x^2 = 0$ with integer lift $a$, then $p^s \mid a^2$, so $2 v_p(a) \geq s$, so $v_p(a) \geq s/2$, and since $v_p(a)$ is an integer (or $\infty$), $v_p(a) \geq \kappa$; hence $p^{\kappa} \mid a$ and $x \in p^{\kappa}(\mathbb{Z}/p^s)$.

Finally, multiplication by $p^{\kappa}$ on $\mathbb{Z}/p^s$ has kernel $p^{s - \kappa}(\mathbb{Z}/p^s)$ of order $p^{\kappa}$, so its image has order $p^{s}/p^{\kappa} = p^{\lfloor s/2 \rfloor}$. Thus $N_s(0) = p^{\lfloor s/2 \rfloor}$. $\blacksquare$

## Remarks

**Remark 1 (Divergence from the thesis: glyph loss in the case display — ERRATA E6.5).** In the thesis's display for Lemma 3.2.6, the first line prints "$2p^r$, when $p = 2$," but the intended condition is $p \neq 2$ (the symbol $\neq$ was lost in typesetting/extraction). The corrected statement above assigns $2p^r$ to odd $p$ only.

**Remark 2 (Divergence from the thesis: statement form — ERRATA E6.5).** The thesis states the result in two stages — the count is zero unless $u$ is a quadratic residue mod $p^{s-2r}$, and otherwise it equals $\#\{\tilde{x} \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{s-r} : \tilde{x}^2 \equiv 1 \bmod p^{s-2r}\}$ — i.e. it factors through a *chosen square root* of $u$. Two hygiene problems: (1) for $p = 2$, the condition that $u$ be a QR mod $2^{s-2r}$ must be parsed as $u \equiv 1 \bmod \min(2^{s-2r}, 8)$ — being a square modulo $2^t$ stabilizes at the modulus $8$ (Step 2(f)), so the naive reading ($u$ is a square mod $2^{s-2r}$) is only correct because of that stabilization, which the thesis leaves implicit; (2) the two-stage formulation obscures that the count is a function of $c = p^{2r}u$ alone. The corrected statement gives the solution counts directly, with explicit congruence conditions on $u$. (The thesis's two-stage count is consistent with ours: when $u$ is a square, $\#\{\tilde{x} \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{s-r} : \tilde{x}^2 \equiv 1 \bmod p^{s-2r}\} = p^r \cdot N_{s-2r}(1)$, which matches ($*$) since $N_{s-2r}(u) = N_{s-2r}(1)$ for square units $u$.)

**Remark 3 (CRITICAL: the residue condition is on $s - 2r$, NOT on $s$ — ERRATA E3).** For $p = 2$, the case split in part (i) is governed by the *difference* $s - 2r$, not by the modulus exponent $s$. This matters precisely when the lemma is applied with $r$ fixed and $s$ varying, which is exactly how §3.3 of the thesis uses it: there $s = 2k - m$ runs over a range as $k$ does (one value of $k$ per stratum of ideals of index $p^m$ with minimal integer $p^k$), while $r$ is fixed at $r = w - 1$ (for $p = 2$, $D$ odd) or $r = w$ (for $p = 2$, $D \equiv 4 \bmod 8$), with $u \equiv D \pmod 8$ resp. $u = (D/4)(f/2^w)^2 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$. The thesis's proof of Theorem 3.1.2 tests the conditions $s - 2r \in \{1, 2\}$ as if they read $2k - m \in \{1, 2\}$ — the two readings coincide **only when** $r = 0$, i.e. $w = 1$ in the $D\text{-odd}$ cases. For $w \geq 2$ (D odd) the term at $k$ with $(2k-m) - 2r \in \{1, 2\}$ — which contributes $2^{w-1}$ ($m$ odd) resp. $2^{w}$ ($m$ even) additional roots for **every** $w$ — was silently dropped, and for $D \equiv 4 \bmod 8$, $m$ odd, the term with $(2k-m)-2r = 1$ (count $2^w$) was likewise dropped. This single misreading is the root cause of the two false 2-adic cases of thesis Theorem 3.1.2 (ERRATA E1: inert, $D \equiv 5 \bmod 8$, $w \geq 2$; ERRATA E2: $D \equiv 4 \bmod 8$, $m$ odd), verified by explicit counterexamples at $d = -48, -192, -16$. With the counts as stated here, the 2-adic case of Theorem 3.1.2 coincides with the odd-$p$ formulas evaluated at $p = 2$ (ERRATA E4). Any consumer of this lemma must bind $r$ and $s$ explicitly before case-splitting.

**Remark 4 (Completion relative to the thesis).** Thesis Lemma 3.2.6 states only part (i) (even valuation, $2r < s$). Parts (ii) and (iii) appear in the thesis only as inline arguments inside the proof of Theorem 3.1.2 (§3.3): the zero case yields the count $p^{k - \lceil m/2 \rceil} = p^{\lfloor (2k-m)/2\rfloor}$ for the strata with $2k - m \leq v_p(d/4)$, and the odd-valuation case underlies the ramified-case claim that $x^2 \equiv d/4$ has no roots ($v_p(x^2)$ is even but $v_p(d/4)$ would be odd). We promote both to explicit parts of the lemma, since the formalization needs them as standalone declarations and the exhaustiveness observation above shows the three parts jointly compute $N_s(c)$ for every $c$.

**Remark 5 (Structure of the unit group).** The standard structure theorems — $(\mathbb{Z}/p^t)^\times$ cyclic for odd $p$, and $(\mathbb{Z}/2^t)^\times = \{\pm 1\} \times \langle 5 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2^{t-2}$ for $t \geq 3$ — imply Steps 2(a)–(f) immediately (for odd $p$: a cyclic group of even order has exactly two elements of order dividing $2$ and its squares form the index-$2$ subgroup; for $p = 2$: the squaring kernel is $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$ of order 4). The proof given above deliberately avoids invoking the structure theorems, using only the elementary kernel/image computations; this matches the intended formalization route (`ZMod.isCyclic_units_of_prime_pow` is available in Mathlib for the odd case, and the explicit $\{\pm 1, 2^{t-1} \pm 1\}$ kernel computation is self-contained for $p = 2$).

**Remark 6 (Numerical sanity checks).** $s = 4$, $p = 2$, $r = 1$, $u = 1$ ($s - 2r = 2$, $u \equiv 1 \bmod 4$): $x^2 \equiv 4 \pmod{16}$ has solutions $x \in \{2, 6, 10, 14\}$, count $4 = 2^{r+1}$. $s = 3$, $r = 0$, $u = 3$: $x^2 \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ has no solutions ($u \not\equiv 1 \bmod 8$). $s = 3$, $r = 1$ ($s - 2r = 1$): $x^2 \equiv 4u \pmod 8$ has solutions $\{2, 6\}$, count $2 = 2^r$, for every odd $u$. $s = 3$, $c = 0$: $x^2 \equiv 0 \pmod 8$ has solutions $\{0, 4\}$, count $2 = 2^{\lfloor 3/2\rfloor}$. Each of these becomes a `decide`/`#eval` test vector in Lean.
