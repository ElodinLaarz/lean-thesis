# Lemma 3.2.2: the localization at the unique prime over $p$

## Statement

**Notation.** Throughout, $d < 0$ is an imaginary quadratic discriminant, i.e. $d \equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod 4$, and we write $d = D f^2$ where $D$ is the (fundamental) discriminant of the field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ and $f \ge 1$ is the conductor. Let

$$\mathcal{O} = \mathcal{O}_d = \mathbb{Z}[\tau], \qquad \tau = \frac{d + \sqrt{d}}{2},$$

the quadratic order of discriminant $d$; $\{1, \tau\}$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\mathcal{O}$. The element $\tau$ has minimal polynomial

$$g(x) = x^2 - d\,x + \frac{d^2 - d}{4} \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$$

(the constant term is an integer because $d \equiv 0, 1 \pmod 4$), so $\mathcal{O} \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(g)$. Conjugation $\alpha \mapsto \bar\alpha$ is the nontrivial automorphism of $K$; since $\bar\tau = d - \tau \in \mathcal{O}$, it restricts to a ring automorphism of $\mathcal{O}$. The norm is $N(\alpha) = \alpha\bar\alpha$; explicitly, for $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}$,

$$N(x + y\tau) = x^2 + d\,xy + \frac{d^2-d}{4}\,y^2 \in \mathbb{Z},$$

multiplicative in $\alpha$, and positive for $\alpha \neq 0$ since $d < 0$. For a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$ we write $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = \#(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a})$ for the ideal index (= ideal norm). For a rational prime $p$, $v_p$ denotes the $p$-adic valuation, $w := v_p(f)$, and $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \{a/t \in \mathbb{Q} : a, t \in \mathbb{Z},\ p \nmid t\}$ is $\mathbb{Z}$ localized at $p$. For a prime ideal $\mathfrak{P} \subset \mathcal{O}$, the localization is

$$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P} := \left\{ \alpha/\beta : \alpha \in \mathcal{O},\ \beta \in \mathcal{O} \smallsetminus \mathfrak{P} \right\} \subseteq K,$$

which is well defined as a subring of $K = \operatorname{Frac}(\mathcal{O})$ because $\mathcal{O}$ is a domain. A prime ideal $\mathfrak{P} \subset \mathcal{O}$ *lies over* $p$ when $\mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p\mathbb{Z}$; equivalently (see Step 0 of the proof), when $p \in \mathfrak{P}$ and $\mathfrak{P}$ is prime.

**Standing hypothesis** (made explicit per ERRATA E6.2): $p$ is a rational prime dividing the conductor, $p \mid f$ (so $w \ge 1$).

> **Lemma 3.2.2.** Assume $p \mid f$. Then:
>
> **(0)** $\mathcal{O}$ has *exactly one* prime ideal $\mathfrak{P}$ lying over $p$, namely $\mathfrak{P} = (p,\ \tau - A_0)$, where $x - A_0$ is the unique monic irreducible factor of $g \bmod p$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$ (one may take $A_0 = 0$ for $p$ odd, and $A_0 \equiv d/4 \pmod 2$ for $p = 2$). In particular $p$ is not split in $\mathcal{O}$, and $\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P} \cong \mathbb{F}_p$.
>
> **(i)** *(Integer denominators.)* Every $z \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ can be written $z = \alpha/t$ with $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}$, $t \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $p \nmid t$. Equivalently, inside $K$,
> $$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P} \;=\; \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau] \;=\; \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} + \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\,\tau .$$
>
> **(ii)** *(Unit criterion.)* For $\alpha = x + y\tau \in \mathcal{O}$, the image of $\alpha$ in $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ is a unit if and only if
> $$p \nmid N(\alpha) = x^2 + d\,xy + \tfrac{d^2-d}{4}\,y^2 \quad (\text{in } \mathbb{Z}).$$

## Role in the development

This lemma is the gateway from global to local in Chapter 3: it identifies the local ring $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ concretely as the rank-2 $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$-module $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} + \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\tau$ and gives a purely arithmetic unit test, which together substitute for the valuation theory that is unavailable because $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ is not a DVR (Remark 4). It is consumed by Lemma 3.2.7 (the minimal positive integer in $p^r(\tau - a)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$; the letter $s$ is reserved for the Lemma 3.2.6 modulus exponent per ERRATA E6.7) and Lemma 3.2.8 (when two such local principal ideals coincide), which drive the invertible-ideal count of Corollary 3.1.3 in §3.3. Its only input is Proposition 3.2.1 (primes of $\mathcal{O}$ over $p$ correspond to the monic irreducible factors of $g \bmod p$), from which the uniqueness (0) is derived under $p \mid f$.

## Proof

**Step 0: uniqueness of the prime over $p$.** By Proposition 3.2.1 (label `prop-3-2-1`), the primes of $\mathcal{O}$ containing $p$ are exactly the ideals $p\mathcal{O} + (g_i(\tau))$, where $\bar g_i$ runs over the monic irreducible factors of $\bar g := g \bmod p$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$ and $g_i \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ is any lift of $\bar g_i$. We claim that under $p \mid f$ the reduction $\bar g$ is the square of a monic linear polynomial:

* *Case $p$ odd.* $p \mid f$ gives $p^2 \mid f^2 \mid d$, so $d \equiv 0 \pmod p$; moreover $v_p\!\big(\tfrac{d^2-d}{4}\big) = v_p\big(d(d-1)\big) = v_p(d) \ge 2$, using that $p$ is odd (so $v_p(4) = 0$) and $p \nmid d - 1$. Hence $\bar g = x^2$.
* *Case $p = 2$.* $2 \mid f$ gives $4 \mid f^2 \mid d$, so $d/4 \in \mathbb{Z}$ and the linear term $-dx$ vanishes mod $2$. The constant term is $\tfrac{d^2 - d}{4} = \tfrac{d}{4}(d-1) \equiv \tfrac{d}{4} \pmod 2$ since $d - 1$ is odd. Thus $\bar g = x^2$ if $8 \mid d$, and $\bar g = x^2 + 1 = (x+1)^2$ if $v_2(d) = 2$. Either way $\bar g$ is the square of a monic linear polynomial.

So $\bar g = (x - \bar A_0)^2$ with $A_0$ as in the statement, $\bar g$ has exactly one monic irreducible factor, and therefore

$$\mathfrak{P} := (p,\ \tau - A_0)$$

is the unique prime of $\mathcal{O}$ containing $p$. Its residue ring is $\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P} \cong \mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x - \bar A_0) \cong \mathbb{F}_p$. Finally, "containing $p$" and "lying over $p$" agree: if $p \in \mathfrak{P}$ then $\mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z}$ is a proper ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ (as $1 \notin \mathfrak{P}$) containing the maximal ideal $p\mathbb{Z}$, whence $\mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p\mathbb{Z}$. This proves (0). In particular $p$ is not split in $\mathcal{O}$ (splitness means two distinct primes over $p$, i.e. two distinct linear factors of $\bar g$).

**Step 1: $\mathfrak{P}$ is stable under conjugation.** Conjugation $\iota : \alpha \mapsto \bar\alpha$ is a ring automorphism of $\mathcal{O}$ (it is the restriction of a field automorphism of $K$, it is involutive, and it maps $\mathcal{O}$ into itself because $\iota(x + y\tau) = (x + yd) - y\tau \in \mathcal{O}$). Hence $\iota(\mathfrak{P})$ is again a prime ideal of $\mathcal{O}$, and it contains $\iota(p) = p$. By the uniqueness in Step 0,

$$\overline{\mathfrak{P}} := \iota(\mathfrak{P}) = \mathfrak{P}.$$

**Step 2: membership in $\mathfrak{P}$ is detected by the norm.** *Claim: for $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}$, $\ \alpha \in \mathfrak{P} \iff p \mid N(\alpha)$.*

($\Rightarrow$) If $\alpha \in \mathfrak{P}$, then $\bar\alpha \in \iota(\mathfrak{P}) = \mathfrak{P}$ by Step 1, so $N(\alpha) = \alpha\bar\alpha \in \mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p\mathbb{Z}$ (Step 0).

($\Leftarrow$) If $p \mid N(\alpha)$, then $\alpha\bar\alpha = N(\alpha) \in p\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathfrak{P}$. Since $\mathfrak{P}$ is prime, $\alpha \in \mathfrak{P}$ or $\bar\alpha \in \mathfrak{P}$. In the latter case, applying $\iota$ gives $\alpha = \iota(\bar\alpha) \in \iota(\mathfrak{P}) = \mathfrak{P}$. Either way $\alpha \in \mathfrak{P}$. This proves the claim. (For $\alpha = 0$: $N(0) = 0$ is divisible by $p$ and $0 \in \mathfrak{P}$, consistently.)

**Step 3: proof of (ii).** Since $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P} \subseteq K$ and $K$ is a field, an element $z \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ if and only if $z \neq 0$ and $z^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$. We show: for $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}$, the image of $\alpha$ in $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ is a unit $\iff \alpha \notin \mathfrak{P}$.

* If $\alpha \notin \mathfrak{P}$, then $\alpha \neq 0$ (as $0 \in \mathfrak{P}$) and $1/\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ by the very definition of the localization, so $\alpha$ is a unit.
* If $\alpha \in \mathfrak{P}$ and $\alpha$ were a unit, write $1/\alpha = \gamma/\delta$ with $\gamma \in \mathcal{O}$, $\delta \in \mathcal{O} \smallsetminus \mathfrak{P}$; cross-multiplying in $K$ gives $\delta = \alpha\gamma \in \mathfrak{P}$, a contradiction.

Combining with Step 2: $\alpha$ is a unit in $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P} \iff \alpha \notin \mathfrak{P} \iff p \nmid N(\alpha)$. Writing $\alpha = x + y\tau$ and expanding $N(\alpha) = x^2 + dxy + \frac{d^2-d}{4}y^2$ gives (ii). $\square$

**Step 4: proof of (i).** First the easy inclusion. If $t \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $p \nmid t$, then $t \notin \mathfrak{P}$ (else $t \in \mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p\mathbb{Z}$), so $\alpha/t \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ for every $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}$. This shows

$$\{\alpha/t : \alpha \in \mathcal{O},\ t \in \mathbb{Z},\ p \nmid t\} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P},$$

and this direction needs only $\mathfrak{P} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p\mathbb{Z}$ (it holds for any prime over any $p$, split or not).

For the reverse inclusion — the content of the lemma — let $z \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ and write $z = \alpha/\beta$ with $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}$, $\beta \in \mathcal{O} \smallsetminus \mathfrak{P}$. By Step 2, $p \nmid N(\beta)$; also $N(\beta) \neq 0$ since $\beta \neq 0$. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $\bar\beta \in \mathcal{O}$,

$$z \;=\; \frac{\alpha}{\beta} \;=\; \frac{\alpha\bar\beta}{\beta\bar\beta} \;=\; \frac{\alpha\bar\beta}{N(\beta)},$$

which is of the required shape with numerator $\alpha\bar\beta \in \mathcal{O}$ and integer denominator $t = N(\beta)$, $p \nmid t$.

Finally, the identification with $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$. On the one hand, bringing coordinates to a common denominator,

$$\{\alpha/t : \alpha \in \mathcal{O},\ t \in \mathbb{Z},\ p \nmid t\} = \left\{\tfrac{x}{t} + \tfrac{y}{t}\tau : x, y, t \in \mathbb{Z},\ p \nmid t\right\} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} + \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\tau.$$

On the other hand, $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$, the $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$-subalgebra of $K$ generated by $\tau$, equals $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} + \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\tau$ because $\tau$ satisfies the monic quadratic $g$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, so all higher powers of $\tau$ reduce. This proves (i). $\blacksquare$

## Remarks

**1. Divergence from the thesis (statement).** The thesis states the lemma for an *arbitrary* prime $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$ over a rational prime $p$: the containment $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \supseteq \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$ always, *with equality when $p$ is not split in $\mathcal{O}$*, and the unit criterion in the non-split case. (Note that by Proposition 3.2.1, "$p$ not split in $\mathcal{O}$" is equivalent to "$p$ has a unique prime of $\mathcal{O}$ over it", which is exactly what the thesis proof uses.) Moreover, in the thesis the §3.2 standing hypothesis "$p \mid f$" is introduced only *after* Lemma 3.2.2 and Remark 3.2.3, although Lemmas 3.2.7 and 3.2.8 silently rely on it; ERRATA E6.2 flags this as a formalization blocker. Following the errata, we state the lemma directly under the hypothesis $p \mid f$ — the only case used in the sequel — under which non-splitness is not assumed but *proved* (Step 0), and the prime $\mathfrak{P}$ is pinned down explicitly. The general-prime containment of the thesis version survives verbatim as the easy inclusion in Step 4.

**2. The statement is FALSE for split $p$.** Both (i) and (ii) fail without the non-split hypothesis; this is why the formal statement must carry $p \mid f$ (or at least uniqueness of the prime over $p$). Counterexample: $d = D = -4$, $f = 1$, $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z}[i]$, $p = 5 = (2+i)(2-i)$, $\mathfrak{P} = (2+i)$.

* *(ii) fails:* $N(2 - i) = 5 \equiv 0 \pmod 5$, yet $2 - i \notin \mathfrak{P}$ (if $2-i = (2+i)\gamma$ then taking norms $5 = 5N(\gamma)$ forces $\gamma \in \{\pm 1, \pm i\}$, and none of $\pm(2+i), \pm(2i - 1)$ equals $2 - i$), so $2 - i$ *is* a unit of $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ despite $5 \mid N(2-i)$.
* *(i) fails:* $z = \frac{1}{2-i} = \frac{2+i}{5} \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ (denominator $2 - i \notin \mathfrak{P}$), but $z$ admits no representation $\alpha/t$ with $\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}[i]$, $t \in \mathbb{Z}$, $5 \nmid t$: cross-multiplying, $t(2+i) = 5\alpha = (2+i)(2-i)\alpha$, so $t = (2-i)\alpha$; taking norms, $t^2 = 5\,N(\alpha)$, hence $5 \mid t$ — contradiction.

Under the standing hypothesis $p \mid f$ the split case can never occur (Step 0). The general dichotomy — a prime splits in $\mathcal{O}$ exactly when it splits in $\mathcal{O}_K$ and is coprime to $f$ — is the thesis's Remark 3.2.3, asserted there without proof; ERRATA E6.3 requires it to become its own blueprint lemma, and Step 0 above is precisely its "$p \mid f$ half".

**3. Divergence from the thesis (proof).** The thesis proves the key fact "$\beta \notin \mathfrak{P} \Rightarrow p \nmid N(\beta)$" by factoring the principal ideal $\beta\mathcal{O}$ into its primary components: $N(\beta) = [\mathcal{O} : \beta\mathcal{O}] = \prod_\mathfrak{q} [\mathcal{O} : \beta\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{q} \cap \mathcal{O}]$, with each local index a power of the rational prime under $\mathfrak{q}$, and then invoking the uniqueness of the prime over $p$ to conclude that no factor is a power of $p$. That argument is correct but needs three auxiliary facts: (a) $N(\beta) = [\mathcal{O} : \beta\mathcal{O}]$; (b) primary decomposition of $\beta\mathcal{O}$ and multiplicativity of the index over comaximal components; (c) $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}]$ is a power of $q$ for $\mathfrak{q}$-primary $\mathfrak{a}$ with $\mathfrak{q}$ over $q$. Our Step 2 replaces all of this with a two-line argument from the conjugation-stability of $\mathfrak{P}$ (Step 1): if $p \mid N(\beta)$ then $\beta\bar\beta \in \mathfrak{P}$, so primality plus $\overline{\mathfrak{P}} = \mathfrak{P}$ force $\beta \in \mathfrak{P}$. Both routes rest on the same pivot — uniqueness of the prime over $p$ — but the conjugation route is elementary and is the shape recommended here for the Lean proof. (The WP-D stub header of `Localization/Basic.lean` currently sketches the thesis's local-index strategy; either route discharges the frozen statements.) (It also proves slightly more: the exact equivalence $\alpha \in \mathfrak{P} \iff p \mid N(\alpha)$, which is what (ii) needs.)

**4. $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ is not a DVR.** Since $p \mid f$, the local ring $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ is a one-dimensional Noetherian local domain that is *not* integrally closed, hence not a DVR: the element $\tau_K = (D + \sqrt{D})/2$, integral over $\mathbb{Z}$ (it is a root of $x^2 - Dx + \frac{D^2 - D}{4}$), lies in $K$ but not in $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} + \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\tau$; indeed $\sqrt{d} = f\sqrt{D}$ gives $\tau_K = \frac{D}{2} - \frac{d}{2f} + \frac{1}{f}\tau$, and the $\tau$-coordinate $1/f$ has $v_p(1/f) = -w < 0$, so $\tau_K \notin \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} + \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\tau$ by uniqueness of coordinates in the $\mathbb{Q}$-basis $\{1, \tau\}$ of $K$. This is why §3.2 works with the explicit module description of $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{P}$ from (i) instead of valuations — the thesis remarks on exactly this obstruction before Theorem 3.1.2.

**5. Scope of the hypothesis.** Only $p \mid f$ (i.e. $w \ge 1$) is used here; the precise value $w = v_p(f)$ becomes relevant starting with Lemma 3.2.6 and the counting results (Theorem 3.1.2, Corollary 3.1.3).
