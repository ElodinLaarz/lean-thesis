# Lemma 3.2.4 — the normal-form bijection (with Remark 3.2.5 and bridge lemma E6.4b)

## Statement

**Notation.** Throughout, $d < 0$ is an integer with $d \equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod 4$, written
$d = D f^2$ with $D$ a fundamental discriminant and $f \geq 1$ the conductor. Let

$$\mathcal{O} \;=\; \mathcal{O}_d \;=\; \mathbb{Z}[\tau], \qquad \tau \;=\; \frac{d + \sqrt{d}}{2}, \qquad g(x) \;=\; x^2 - d\,x + \frac{d^2 - d}{4} \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}[x].$$

The polynomial $g$ has integer coefficients because $4 \mid d^2 - d$ for $d \equiv 0, 1 \pmod 4$; it is the minimal polynomial of $\tau$ (one checks $\tau^2 = d\tau - \tfrac{d^2-d}{4}$), and $\operatorname{disc} g = d^2 - (d^2 - d) = d$. Thus $\mathcal{O} \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(g)$ is the imaginary quadratic order of discriminant $d$, a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module with basis $(1, \tau)$. Writing $\bar\tau = \frac{d - \sqrt d}{2}$ for the conjugate, we have $\tau + \bar\tau = d$, $\tau\bar\tau = \tfrac{d^2-d}{4}$, and for $A \in \mathbb{Z}$

$$N(\tau - A) \;=\; (\tau - A)(\bar\tau - A) \;=\; A^2 - dA + \tfrac{d^2-d}{4} \;=\; g(A).$$

For a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$ of finite index, the **index** (= ideal norm) is $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] := \#(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a})$, the index of $\mathfrak{a}$ as an additive subgroup of $\mathcal{O} \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ (see the index infrastructure, [infra-index]). Fix a rational prime $p$ with $p \mid f$ and set $w := v_p(f)$ (the standing hypothesis of thesis §3.2; $w$ itself is not used in this result). By Proposition 3.2.1 together with the fact that $\bar g := g \bmod p$ is the square of a linear polynomial when $p \mid f$ (see [prop-3-2-1]), there is a **unique** prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $\mathcal{O}$ above $p$, namely $\mathfrak{p} = (p, \tau - c)$ where $c$ is the double root of $\bar g$. Recall that a proper ideal $\mathfrak{q} \subsetneq \mathcal{O}$ is *$\mathfrak{p}$-primary* if $xy \in \mathfrak{q}$ implies $x \in \mathfrak{q}$ or $y \in \sqrt{\mathfrak{q}}$, and $\sqrt{\mathfrak{q}} = \mathfrak{p}$.

For integers $k \geq 0$ and $A$, and a fixed exponent $m \geq 0$, define the **canonical ideal**

$$\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} \;:=\; \bigl(p^k,\; p^{m-k}(\tau - A)\bigr) \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{O}.$$

Call the pair $(k, A)$ **admissible** (for $p$ and $m$) if

$$\lceil m/2 \rceil \;\leq\; k \;\leq\; m \qquad\text{and}\qquad p^{2k-m} \mid g(A).$$

(For integer $k$ the condition $\lceil m/2\rceil \le k$ is equivalent to the thesis's $m/2 \le k$, i.e. to $m \le 2k$; so for an admissible pair both exponents are nonnegative — $2k - m \ge 0$ from $\lceil m/2\rceil \le k$, and $m - k \ge 0$ from $k \le m$.)

> **Lemma 3.2.4 (corrected normal-form bijection; includes Remark 3.2.5 and bridge lemma E6.4b).**
> Fix $m \geq 0$ and $p \mid f$ as above. Then:
>
> **(i) ($\mathbb{Z}$-spans suffice — Remark 3.2.5.)** For admissible $(k, A)$,
> $$\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} \;=\; \mathbb{Z}\,p^k \,+\, \mathbb{Z}\,p^{m-k}(\tau - A) :$$
> every element of the ideal is already a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of the two generators.
>
> **(ii) (Index.)** For admissible $(k, A)$: $\;[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}] = p^m$.
>
> **(iii) (Minimality of the $p$-power — bridge lemma, ERRATA E6.4b.)** For admissible $(k, A)$:
> $$\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} \cap \mathbb{Z} \;=\; p^k\,\mathbb{Z}.$$
> In particular $p^k \in \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$, and if $k \geq 1$ then $p^{k-1} \notin \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$; that is, $k = \min\{\, j \geq 0 : p^j \in \mathfrak{a}_{k,A} \,\}$.
>
> **(iv) (Existence of the normal form.)** Every ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$ with $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = p^m$ equals $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ for some admissible pair $(k, A)$, where necessarily $k = \min\{\, j \geq 0 : p^j \in \mathfrak{a} \,\}$.
>
> **(v) (Uniqueness.)** For admissible pairs $(k, A)$ and $(k', A')$ (same $m$):
> $$\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} = \mathfrak{a}_{k',A'} \iff k = k' \ \text{ and } \ A \equiv A' \pmod{p^{2k-m}}.$$
>
> Consequently the assignment $(k, [A]) \mapsto \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ is a **bijection**
> $$\bigl\{\, (k, [A]) \;:\; \lceil m/2\rceil \leq k \leq m, \ [A] \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{2k-m}\mathbb{Z}, \ g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k-m} \,\bigr\} \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \bigl\{\, \mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O} \;:\; [\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = p^m \,\bigr\},$$
> and therefore the **counting formula** holds:
> $$\#\bigl\{\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O} : [\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = p^m\bigr\} \;=\; \sum_{k = \lceil m/2\rceil}^{m} \#\bigl\{\, A \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{2k-m}\mathbb{Z} \;:\; g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k-m} \,\bigr\}.$$
> Moreover, for $m \geq 1$ the ideals of index $p^m$ are precisely the $\mathfrak{p}$-primary ideals of norm $p^m$ (bridge lemma (a) of [infra-index]), so the bijection and the count also describe the $\mathfrak{p}$-primary ideals of norm $p^m$, as in the thesis's formulation.

The conditions in the source set are well defined on residue classes $[A] \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{2k-m}\mathbb{Z}$: since $g \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, whether $p^{2k-m} \mid g(A)$ depends only on $A \bmod p^{2k-m}$.

## Role in the development

This is the keystone of Chapter 3. Theorem 3.1.2\* (the corrected, $p$-uniform count of all ideals of norm $p^m$) is proved by evaluating the right-hand side of the counting formula: one completes the square ($x \mapsto x + d/2$, with a $2$-adic variant) and applies the root-counting Lemma 3.2.6 for each $k$, then sums. Corollary 3.1.3\* (invertible ideals) counts a subfamily inside this same parametrization, and Lemma 3.2.8's uniqueness clause delegates to part (v). Upstream, this document uses: the standing notation [notation]; the unique prime $\mathfrak{p}$ above $p \mid f$ [prop-3-2-1]; and the index infrastructure [infra-index] (the definition of the index as an additive-subgroup index, Lagrange's theorem in $\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a}$, and bridge lemma (a): an ideal of index $p^m$, $m \geq 1$, is $\mathfrak{p}$-primary); the elementary index computation for the triangular pair of generators is carried out inline in Lemma B.

## Proof

### Step 0 — Coordinates, and the quotient ring $R_k$

$\mathcal{O}$ is free over $\mathbb{Z}$ with basis $(1, \tau)$; we write elements in coordinates $\alpha = c_0 + c_1 \tau$, $c_0, c_1 \in \mathbb{Z}$, and use throughout that these coordinates are unique. Multiplication is determined by

$$\tau^2 \;=\; d\,\tau - q, \qquad q := \tfrac{d^2 - d}{4} \in \mathbb{Z}. \tag{0.1}$$

For $k \geq 1$ set

$$R_k \;:=\; (\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})[x]\,/\,(\bar g), \qquad \bar g \;=\; g \bmod p^k .$$

Since $\bar g$ is monic of degree $2$, Euclidean division by $\bar g$ is valid over the ring $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$, so every class in $R_k$ has a *unique* representative $c_1 x + c_0$ with $c_0, c_1 \in \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$. Hence $R_k$ is a free $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$-module with basis $(1, \bar x)$; in particular

$$\# R_k = p^{2k}, \tag{0.2}$$

and the structure map $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z} \to R_k$ is injective; we call its image the **constants** of $R_k$. The surjection

$$\pi \;:\; \mathcal{O} \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(g) \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}[x]/(g, p^k) \cong R_k, \qquad \tau \mapsto \bar x,$$

has kernel $p^k \mathcal{O}$, and $\pi$ maps coordinates to coordinates mod $p^k$.

Finally, recall the elementary valuation structure of $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$: every nonzero $c \in \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ can be written $c = u\,p^{v(c)}$ with $u$ a unit and $v(c) \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}$ uniquely determined (namely $v(c) = v_p(\tilde c)$ for any lift $\tilde c$ of $c$ with $v_p(\tilde c) < k$).

### Lemma A ($\mathbb{Z}$-spans suffice — part (i); Remark 3.2.5; Lean `canonicalIdeal_eq_zSpan`)

*Let $(k, A)$ be admissible. Then $L := \mathbb{Z}\,p^k + \mathbb{Z}\,p^{m-k}(\tau - A)$ equals the ideal $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$.*

**Proof.** Clearly $L \subseteq \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$. For the converse it suffices to show that $L$ is an ideal of $\mathcal{O}$; since it contains both generators of $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$, it then contains the ideal they generate. $L$ is an additive subgroup, and $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z} + \mathbb{Z}\tau$, so $L$ is an ideal as soon as $\tau L \subseteq L$; by additivity it is enough to check $\tau$ times each generator. Write $g(A) = C\,p^{2k-m}$ with $C \in \mathbb{Z}$ (admissibility). Both exponents $2k - m$ and $m - k$ are $\geq 0$ (admissibility again). Then:

$$\tau \cdot p^k \;=\; A\,p^k \;+\; p^{2k-m}\cdot\bigl[p^{m-k}(\tau - A)\bigr], \tag{A.1}$$

since $A p^k + p^{2k-m} p^{m-k}(\tau - A) = A p^k + p^k(\tau - A) = p^k \tau$. And, using $(0.1)$:

$$\tau \cdot p^{m-k}(\tau - A) \;=\; p^{m-k}\bigl(\tau^2 - A\tau\bigr) \;=\; p^{m-k}\bigl((d - A)\tau - q\bigr).$$

Now $(d - A)\tau - q = (d - A)(\tau - A) + (d-A)A - q = (d - A)(\tau - A) - g(A)$, because $g(A) = A^2 - dA + q = q - (d - A)A$. Hence

$$\tau \cdot p^{m-k}(\tau - A) \;=\; (d - A)\cdot\bigl[p^{m-k}(\tau - A)\bigr] \;-\; C\,p^k, \tag{A.2}$$

using $p^{m-k} g(A) = C\,p^{m-k}p^{2k-m} = C\,p^k$. Both right-hand sides are $\mathbb{Z}$-combinations of the two generators, so $\tau L \subseteq L$. $\blacksquare$

*(Identities (A.1)–(A.2) are exactly the ones proved in the existing Lean development; the coefficient $-C$ is the negated admissibility witness. The thesis's version of this computation, Remark 3.2.5, contains sign slips fixed here; see the Remarks.)*

### Lemma B (Index — part (ii); Lean `idealIndex_canonicalIdeal`)

*Let $(k, A)$ be admissible. Then $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}] = p^m$.*

**Proof.** By Lemma A, $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ is the subgroup of $\mathcal{O} \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ generated by the two vectors with coordinates (in the basis $(1, \tau)$)

$$p^k \;\longleftrightarrow\; (p^k,\, 0), \qquad p^{m-k}(\tau - A) \;\longleftrightarrow\; \bigl(-A\,p^{m-k},\; p^{m-k}\bigr),$$

i.e. by the columns of the integer matrix

$$M \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} p^k & -A\,p^{m-k} \\ 0 & p^{m-k} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det M = p^k \cdot p^{m-k} = p^m \neq 0 .$$

Since $M$ is triangular, the index of its column span is computed directly. For $(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$: $(x, y) = a\,(p^k, 0) + b\,(-A\,p^{m-k}, p^{m-k})$ for some $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$ if and only if $p^{m-k} \mid y$ and $p^k \mid x + Ay$ (necessarily $b = y/p^{m-k}$, and the first coordinate then reads $x = a\,p^k - Ay$, i.e. $x + Ay = a\,p^k$). Hence $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ is exactly the kernel of the group homomorphism

$$\mathbb{Z}^2 \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/p^{m-k}\mathbb{Z}, \qquad (x, y) \longmapsto \bigl(x + Ay \bmod p^k,\ y \bmod p^{m-k}\bigr),$$

which is surjective because $(x, y) \mapsto (x + Ay, y)$ is a bijection of $\mathbb{Z}^2$. Therefore

$$[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}] \;=\; p^k \cdot p^{m-k} \;=\; p^m \;=\; |\det M|.$$

(The determinant formulation is how the Lean proof phrases it; [infra-index] supplies only the definition of the index as an additive-subgroup index.) $\blacksquare$

### Lemma C (Minimality of the $p$-power — part (iii); bridge lemma E6.4b; Lean `canonicalIdeal_minimal_pow`)

*Let $(k, A)$ be admissible. Then $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p^k \mathbb{Z}$. In particular the minimal $j \geq 0$ with $p^j \in \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ is exactly $k$.*

**Proof.** By Lemma A, a general element of $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ is

$$a\,p^k + b\,p^{m-k}(\tau - A) \;=\; \bigl(a\,p^k - A\,b\,p^{m-k}\bigr) \;+\; \bigl(b\,p^{m-k}\bigr)\,\tau, \qquad a, b \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

By uniqueness of coordinates in the basis $(1, \tau)$, this element lies in $\mathbb{Z}$ if and only if its $\tau$-coordinate vanishes: $b\,p^{m-k} = 0$, i.e. $b = 0$. Thus $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} \cap \mathbb{Z} = \{a p^k : a \in \mathbb{Z}\} = p^k\mathbb{Z}$. Consequently $p^k \in \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$; and $p^j \in \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ forces $p^k \mid p^j$, i.e. $j \geq k$. (For $k \geq 1$ this says in particular $p^{k-1} \notin \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$.) $\blacksquare$

**Why this matters (the E6.4b gap).** The thesis proves that every $\mathfrak{p}$-primary ideal of norm $p^m$ has the form $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ with $k$ *its own* minimal $p$-power exponent, and that for *fixed* $k$ the class $[A]$ is unique — but its converse ("any such ideal is $\mathfrak{p}$-primary of norm $p^m$") does not assert that the *constructed* ideal $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ has minimal $p$-power exactly $p^k$. Without Lemma C, the parameter sets for different $k$ could a priori produce the same ideal, and the §3.3 count $r(p^m) = \sum_k r_k(p^m)$ (where $r_k$ counts ideals whose minimal $p$-power is $p^k$) could double-count. Lemma C closes this gap: distinct $k$ give disjoint families.

### Lemma D (Principal normal form in $R_k$)

*Let $k \geq 1$ and let $\mathfrak{b} \subseteq R_k$ be an ideal containing no nonzero constants (i.e. $\mathfrak{b} \cap (\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})\!\cdot\!1 = 0$). Then there exist an integer $a$ with $0 \leq a \leq k$ and $A \in \mathbb{Z}$ with*

$$g(A) \equiv 0 \pmod{p^{k-a}},$$

*such that, setting $\xi := p^a(\bar x - \bar A) \in R_k$:*

$$\mathfrak{b} \;=\; (\xi) \;=\; (\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})\cdot \xi, \qquad \#\mathfrak{b} = p^{\,k-a}.$$

*(If $a = k$ then $\xi = 0$ and $\mathfrak{b} = 0$; any $A$ works, and $g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^0$ is vacuous.)*

**Proof.** Write elements of $R_k$ in their unique coordinates $\eta = c_1(\eta)\,\bar x + c_0(\eta)$, $c_i(\eta) \in \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ (Step 0).

*Case $\mathfrak{b} = 0$:* take $a = k$, $A = 0$; then $\xi = p^k(\bar x - 0) = 0$ in $R_k$, all claims hold with $\#\mathfrak{b} = 1 = p^{k-k}$.

*Case $\mathfrak{b} \neq 0$:* If every $\eta \in \mathfrak{b}$ had $c_1(\eta) = 0$, then $\mathfrak{b}$ would consist of constants, hence $\mathfrak{b} = 0$ — contradiction. So the set $\{\, v(c_1(\eta)) : \eta \in \mathfrak{b},\ c_1(\eta) \neq 0 \,\} \subseteq \{0, \dots, k-1\}$ is nonempty; let $a$ be its minimum, attained at some $\eta_0 \in \mathfrak{b}$ with $c_1(\eta_0) = u\,p^a$, $u$ a unit. Since $\mathfrak{b}$ is an ideal, $\xi_0 := u^{-1}\eta_0 = p^a \bar x + c_0' \in \mathfrak{b}$ for some $c_0' \in \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$.

**(D1) The constant coefficient is divisible by $p^a$.** The element $p^{k-a}\,\xi_0 = p^k\bar x + p^{k-a}c_0' = p^{k-a}c_0' \cdot 1 \in \mathfrak{b}$ is a constant, hence zero: $p^{k-a} c_0' = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$, i.e. $c_0' \in p^a\,(\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})$. Write $c_0' = -p^a \bar A$ for some $A \in \mathbb{Z}$. Thus $\xi := p^a(\bar x - \bar A) = \xi_0 \in \mathfrak{b}$.

**(D2) The root condition.** In $R_k$ we have $\bar x^{\,2} = \bar d\,\bar x - \bar q$ by $(0.1)$, so

$$\bar x\,\xi \;=\; p^a\bigl(\bar x^{\,2} - \bar A \bar x\bigr) \;=\; p^a\bigl((\bar d - \bar A)\bar x - \bar q\bigr) \;=\; (\bar d - \bar A)\,\xi \;-\; p^a\,\bar g(\bar A)\cdot 1,$$

where $\bar g(\bar A) = \bar A^2 - \bar d \bar A + \bar q$ (the same completing-the-product manipulation as in (A.2)). Hence $p^a \bar g(\bar A)\cdot 1 = (\bar d - \bar A)\xi - \bar x \xi \in \mathfrak{b}$ is a constant, so it is zero: $p^a g(A) \equiv 0 \pmod{p^k}$, i.e.

$$g(A) \equiv 0 \pmod{p^{k-a}}. \tag{D.1}$$

**(D3) $\mathfrak{b}$ is the $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$-span of $\xi$.** "$\supseteq$": $\xi \in \mathfrak{b}$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ is an ideal. "$\subseteq$": let $\eta \in \mathfrak{b}$. If $c_1(\eta) = 0$, then $\eta$ is a constant in $\mathfrak{b}$, hence $\eta = 0$. Otherwise $b := v(c_1(\eta)) \geq a$ by minimality of $a$; write $c_1(\eta) = u' p^{b}$ with $u'$ a unit. Then $\eta - u' p^{\,b-a}\,\xi \in \mathfrak{b}$ has $\bar x$-coordinate $u'p^b - u'p^{b-a}p^a = 0$, hence is a constant in $\mathfrak{b}$, hence zero: $\eta = u'p^{\,b-a}\xi \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})\,\xi$. In particular the module span coincides with the ideal $(\xi)$ (consistently with (D2), which shows the span is $\bar x$-stable: $\bar x \xi = (\bar d - \bar A)\xi$ once (D.1) holds).

**(D4) Cardinality.** $\#\mathfrak{b} = \#\bigl((\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})\xi\bigr) = p^k / \#\operatorname{Ann}(\xi)$. For $c \in \mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$: $c\,\xi = (c\,p^a)\bar x - (c\,p^a \bar A)$ vanishes iff its $\bar x$-coordinate $c\,p^a$ vanishes (the constant coordinate then vanishes automatically), iff $c \in p^{k-a}(\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})$, a subgroup of order $p^a$. Hence $\#\mathfrak{b} = p^{k-a}$. $\blacksquare$

### Part (iv): existence of the normal form (Lean `exists_canonicalIdeal_eq`)

Let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$ be an ideal with $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = p^m$.

*Case $m = 0$:* then $\mathfrak{a} = \mathcal{O}$, the pair $(k, A) = (0, 0)$ is admissible ($\lceil 0/2\rceil = 0 \leq 0 \leq 0$ and $p^0 \mid g(0)$), and $\mathfrak{a}_{0,0} = (1, \tau) = \mathcal{O}$; moreover $\min\{j : p^j \in \mathcal{O}\} = 0$. Done.

*Case $m \geq 1$:*

1. **$p^m \in \mathfrak{a}$.** The additive group $\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a}$ has order $p^m$, so by Lagrange $p^m \cdot (1 + \mathfrak{a}) = 0$ in $\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a}$, i.e. $p^m \in \mathfrak{a}$. ([infra-index] packages this.)

2. **Definition of $k$; the bound $k \leq m$; and $k \geq 1$.** $\mathfrak{a} \cap \mathbb{Z}$ is an ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ containing $p^m\mathbb{Z}$, hence equals $n\mathbb{Z}$ with $n \mid p^m$; so $n = p^k$ with $0 \leq k \leq m$, and $k = \min\{j \geq 0 : p^j \in \mathfrak{a}\}$. If $k = 0$ then $1 \in \mathfrak{a}$, contradicting $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = p^m > 1$; so $k \geq 1$.

3. **Reduction mod $p^k$.** Since $p^k \in \mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{a}$ is an ideal, $p^k\mathcal{O} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}$. Let $\mathfrak{b} := \pi(\mathfrak{a}) \subseteq R_k$ with $\pi$ as in Step 0; then $\mathfrak{a} = \pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{b})$ and $R_k/\mathfrak{b} \cong \mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a}$, so by $(0.2)$
   $$\#\mathfrak{b} \;=\; p^{2k - m}, \qquad\text{in particular } p^m \le p^{2k}, \text{ i.e. } m \leq 2k, \text{ i.e. } \lceil m/2\rceil \leq k.$$

4. **No nonzero constants in $\mathfrak{b}$.** For $c \in \mathbb{Z}$: $\pi(c) \in \mathfrak{b}$ iff $c \in \mathfrak{a} + p^k\mathcal{O} = \mathfrak{a}$, iff $c \in \mathfrak{a} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p^k \mathbb{Z}$, iff $\pi(c) = 0$. So $\mathfrak{b}$ contains no nonzero constants.

5. **Apply Lemma D.** There are $0 \leq a \leq k$ and $A \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{k-a}$ such that $\mathfrak{b} = (p^a(\bar x - \bar A))$ and $\#\mathfrak{b} = p^{k-a}$. Comparing with step 3: $p^{k-a} = p^{2k-m}$, hence
   $$a \;=\; m - k.$$
   The root condition becomes $g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k - m}$, and with step 2–3 the pair $(k, A)$ is admissible.

6. **Pull back.** Since $\mathfrak{a} \supseteq \ker\pi = p^k\mathcal{O}$,
   $$\mathfrak{a} \;=\; \pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{b}) \;=\; p^k\mathcal{O} + p^{m-k}(\tau - A)\,\mathcal{O} \;=\; \bigl(p^k, \; p^{m-k}(\tau - A)\bigr) \;=\; \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}. \qquad\blacksquare$$

### Part (v): uniqueness (Lean `canonicalIdeal_inj`)

*($\Leftarrow$)* Suppose $k = k'$ and $A' = A + t\,p^{2k-m}$, $t \in \mathbb{Z}$. Then

$$p^{m-k}(\tau - A') \;=\; p^{m-k}(\tau - A) \;-\; t\,p^{m-k}p^{2k-m} \;=\; p^{m-k}(\tau - A) - t\,p^k,$$

so each generator of $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A'}$ lies in $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$, and symmetrically; hence $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} = \mathfrak{a}_{k,A'}$. (Note also that admissibility depends only on $[A]$: $g(A + tp^{2k-m}) = g(A) + tp^{2k-m}\,(2A + tp^{2k-m} - d) \equiv g(A) \bmod p^{2k-m}$.)

*($\Rightarrow$)* Suppose $\mathfrak{a} := \mathfrak{a}_{k,A} = \mathfrak{a}_{k',A'}$ with both pairs admissible. By Lemma C applied to each presentation,

$$k \;=\; \min\{j : p^j \in \mathfrak{a}\} \;=\; k'.$$

By Lemma A, $p^{m-k}(\tau - A') \in \mathfrak{a}_{k,A} = \mathbb{Z}p^k + \mathbb{Z}p^{m-k}(\tau - A)$: write

$$p^{m-k}(\tau - A') \;=\; a_1\,p^k + a_2\,p^{m-k}(\tau - A), \qquad a_1, a_2 \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

Comparing $\tau$-coordinates: $p^{m-k} = a_2\,p^{m-k}$, so $a_2 = 1$. Comparing constant coordinates: $-A'p^{m-k} = a_1 p^k - A\,p^{m-k}$, so $p^{m-k}(A - A') = a_1 p^k$ and, dividing by $p^{m-k}$,

$$A - A' \;=\; a_1\,p^{2k - m} \;\equiv\; 0 \pmod{p^{2k-m}}. \qquad\blacksquare$$

### The bijection and the counting formula (Lean `idealCount_prime_pow_eq_sum_rootCount`)

Let

$$S_m := \bigl\{ (k, [A]) : \lceil m/2\rceil \le k \le m,\ [A] \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{2k-m}\mathbb{Z},\ g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k-m} \bigr\}, \qquad T_m := \bigl\{ \mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O} : [\mathcal{O}:\mathfrak{a}] = p^m \bigr\},$$

and let $\Phi(k, [A]) := \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$. Then:

- $\Phi$ is **well defined**: the value is independent of the representative $A$ of $[A]$ by (v)($\Leftarrow$), and lands in $T_m$ by Lemma B.
- $\Phi$ is **injective**: if $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} = \mathfrak{a}_{k',A'}$, then $k = k'$ and $[A] = [A']$ by (v)($\Rightarrow$) (which used Lemma C for the equality of the $k$'s).
- $\Phi$ is **surjective** by (iv).

$S_m$ is finite (a finite disjoint union of subsets of finite rings), so both sides are finite and

$$\#T_m \;=\; \#S_m \;=\; \sum_{k=\lceil m/2\rceil}^{m} \#\bigl\{ A \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{2k-m}\mathbb{Z} : g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k-m} \bigr\}.$$

Finally, for $m \geq 1$: every $\mathfrak{a} \in T_m$ is $\mathfrak{p}$-primary by bridge lemma (a) of [infra-index] (its proof uses the uniqueness of the prime $\mathfrak{p}$ above $p$, [prop-3-2-1]); conversely a $\mathfrak{p}$-primary ideal of norm $p^m$ lies in $T_m$ by definition of the norm. So $T_m$ *is* the set of $\mathfrak{p}$-primary ideals of norm $p^m$, recovering the thesis's formulation. $\blacksquare$

**Refinement by minimal $p$-power.** With the thesis's §3.3 notation $r_k(p^m) := \#\{\mathfrak{a} : [\mathcal{O}:\mathfrak{a}] = p^m,\ p^k \in \mathfrak{a},\ p^{k-1} \notin \mathfrak{a}\}$, the bijection $\Phi$ restricts, for each fixed $k$, to a bijection onto the $r_k$-family — by (iii) the image of the stratum $k$ consists of ideals with minimal $p$-power exactly $p^k$, and by (iv) it exhausts them. Hence

$$r_k(p^m) \;=\; \#\bigl\{ A \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{2k-m}\mathbb{Z} : g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k-m} \bigr\},$$

which is exactly the per-$k$ identity that §3.3 sums. This identity is *not* fully proved in the thesis: it needs Lemma C (E6.4b).

## Remarks

### Divergence from the thesis (1): the minimality clause — ERRATA E6.4b

The thesis's converse reads "Conversely, any such ideal is $\mathfrak{p}$-primary of norm $p^m$" and stops there. As stated this does not exclude that $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} = \mathfrak{a}_{k',A'}$ for $k \neq k'$, which would break the decomposition $r(p^m) = \sum_k r_k(p^m)$ used throughout §3.3 (double counting across $k$). The corrected statement adds part (iii) ($\mathfrak{a}_{k,A} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p^k\mathbb{Z}$, so the minimal $p$-power of the *constructed* ideal is exactly $p^k$), proved here from the $\mathbb{Z}$-span description by comparing $\tau$-coordinates. An equivalent route (for $k \geq 1$): by Lemma A the image of $\mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ in $R_k$ is the span $(\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})\,\xi$ with $\xi = p^{m-k}(\bar x - \bar A)$, and this span contains no nonzero constant — a multiple $c\,\xi$ is a constant only when its $\bar x$-coordinate $c\,p^{m-k}$ vanishes, and then its constant coordinate $-c\,p^{m-k}\bar A$ vanishes as well; so $p^{k-1} \in \mathfrak{a}_{k,A}$ would exhibit the nonzero constant $p^{k-1}$ inside that span, a contradiction. The coordinate argument given above is the shorter version of the same computation, and is the one the Lean proof follows.

### Divergence from the thesis (2): sign slips in the two-generator reduction and in Remark 3.2.5 — ERRATA E6.9

Fixed silently in the proof above; for the record: (a) in the thesis's two-generator reduction the generators are introduced as $p^a x + a_0,\ p^b x + b_0$ but the computation is carried out with $p^a x - a_0,\ p^b x - b_0$, and the displayed constant "$p^{b-a}a_0 - b_0$" should be $b_0 - p^{b-a}a_0$ for the generators as introduced; (b) in Remark 3.2.5's display the substitution for $-\tfrac{d^2-d}{4}$ flips sign, absorbed there into an unnamed constant $C$ — in our (A.2) the constant is pinned down as $-C$ with $g(A) = Cp^{2k-m}$. Neither slip affects the argument's structure.

### Divergence from the thesis (3): statement hygiene

(a) The thesis's "there exists a minimal integer $k$ with $m/2 \le k \le m$" is restated as: $k := \min\{j \ge 0 : p^j \in \mathfrak{a}\}$ exists and satisfies $\lceil m/2\rceil \le k \le m$ (for integer $k$, $m/2 \le k \iff \lceil m/2\rceil \le k$).
(b) Remark 3.2.5 is promoted to a standalone lemma, part (i) — it is load-bearing for parts (ii), (iii), (v) and is already formalized (`canonicalIdeal_eq_zSpan`).
(c) The thesis's phrase "we can assume that the $\alpha_i$ are linear" is justified here by Euclidean division by the monic $\bar g$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})[x]$ (Step 0); and the cardinality $\#(p^a(\bar x - \bar A)) = p^{k-a}$, which the thesis computes "by just considering its integer multiples", is carried out via the annihilator computation (D4); the root condition (D.1), derived just before it, is what makes the span $\bar x$-stable — i.e. an ideal, so that the cardinality computed is that of $(\xi)$ — and the thesis derives (D.1) at the same point, so the order of steps is preserved.

### The $m = 0$ corner

For $m = 0$ the unique ideal of index $1$ is $\mathcal{O}$ itself, matched by the unique parameter $(k, [A]) = (0, [0])$ with $[0] \in \mathbb{Z}/p^0\mathbb{Z}$ the trivial class; the counting formula reads $1 = 1$. However, $\mathcal{O}$ is **not** a $\mathfrak{p}$-primary ideal (primary ideals are proper by definition), so the $\mathfrak{p}$-primary phrasing of the result is restricted to $m \geq 1$. The thesis includes $m = 0$ in the primary-ideal phrasing; the bijection and the counting formula are what hold verbatim for all $m \geq 0$, and they are what the Lean formalizes.

### $p$-uniformity and the role of $p \mid f$

No step of this proof distinguishes $p = 2$: the normal-form bijection is $p$-uniform. (The thesis's 2-adic errors E1–E3 arise later, in evaluating the root counts via Lemma 3.2.6, not here.) Moreover the hypothesis $p \mid f$ is used **only** for the $\mathfrak{p}$-primary interpretation (uniqueness of the prime above $p$, and bridge lemma (a)); parts (i)–(v) and the counting formula hold for every rational prime $p$ and every quadratic order $\mathbb{Z}[\tau]$. Sanity check in the maximal-order case $f = 1$: for $p$ split, $g$ has $2$ simple roots mod $p$, hence $2$ roots mod $p^j$ for every $j \geq 1$ (Hensel), so each $k$ with $2k - m \geq 1$ contributes $2$ and the $k = m/2$ term (present only for $m$ even) contributes $1$; in both parities the sum is $m + 1$, matching Proposition 3.1.1.

### Worked check against the ERRATA enumeration data

$d = -16$ ($D = -4$, $f = 2$), $p = 2$, $m = 3$: here $g(x) = x^2 + 16x + 68$. The formula ranges over $k \in \{2, 3\}$. For $k = 2$: $g(A) \equiv A^2 \equiv 0 \bmod 2$ gives the single class $A \equiv 0$, i.e. the ideal $(4, 2\tau)$. For $k = 3$: $g(A) \equiv A^2 + 4 \equiv 0 \bmod 8$ gives $A \equiv 2, 6 \bmod 8$, i.e. $(8, \tau - 2)$ and $(8, \tau - 6)$. Total $3$ — exactly the hand-verified list in ERRATA E2 (which the thesis's Theorem 3.1.2 display contradicts; the *lemma* is correct and is the tool that produces the corrected counts).

### Lean pitfall: natural subtraction

The Lean definitions use $\mathbb{N}$-subtraction: `2 * k - m` and `m - k` truncate at $0$. All statements therefore carry the hypotheses `k ≤ m` and `m ≤ 2 * k` (the admissibility range), exactly the regime in which the truncated exponents agree with the integer ones used here. `CanonicalAdmissible` with `2 * k < m` is a vacuous predicate by design and is never used outside the guarded range.
