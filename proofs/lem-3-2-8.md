# Lemma 3.2.8 — Equality criterion for principal local ideals

## Statement

**Setting and notation.** Throughout, $d < 0$ is a quadratic discriminant: $d \equiv 0$ or
$1 \pmod 4$ and $d$ is not a square (automatic since $d < 0$). Let

$$\mathcal{O} \;=\; \mathcal{O}_d \;=\; \mathbb{Z}[\tau], \qquad \tau \;=\; \frac{d + \sqrt{d}}{2},$$

the imaginary quadratic order of discriminant $d$ inside $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$. The minimal
polynomial of $\tau$ is

$$g(x) \;=\; x^2 - d\,x + \frac{d^2 - d}{4} \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}[x],$$

so $\mathcal{O} \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(g)$. Write $d = D f^2$ with $D$ the fundamental
discriminant of $K$ and $f = \operatorname{cond}(d) \geq 1$ the conductor. Let $\bar{\cdot}$
denote the nontrivial automorphism of $K$; then $\bar\tau = d - \tau$, and Vieta gives
$\tau + \bar\tau = d$, $\tau\bar\tau = \tfrac{d^2-d}{4}$. The norm is $N(\xi) = \xi\bar\xi$; for
an ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$ its norm is the index
$[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = \#(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a})$. For $t \in \mathbb{Z}$,

$$N(\tau - t) \;=\; (\tau - t)(\bar\tau - t) \;=\; \tau\bar\tau - t(\tau + \bar\tau) + t^2 \;=\; t^2 - d\,t + \frac{d^2-d}{4} \;=\; g(t),$$

and $g(t) = (t - d/2)^2 - d/4 > 0$ because $d < 0$; in particular $g(t) \neq 0$, so $v_p(g(t))$
is a well-defined non-negative integer, $v_p$ being the $p\text{-adic}$ valuation.

**Standing hypotheses (§3.2 of the thesis, restated explicitly per ERRATA E6.2).** Fix a
rational prime $p$ **dividing the conductor** $f$, and set $w := v_p(f) \geq 1$. Because
$p \mid f$, the reduction $g \bmod p$ is the square of a linear polynomial, so by Proposition
3.2.1 there is a **unique** prime $\mathfrak{p} \subset \mathcal{O}$ above $p$; in particular
$p$ is non-split in $\mathcal{O}$ (Remark 3.2.3), and Lemma 3.2.2 identifies the localization at
$\mathfrak{p}$ as

$$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \;=\; \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau] \;\subseteq\; K,$$

where $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \{a/s : a, s \in \mathbb{Z},\ p \nmid s\}$. All ideals below are
ideals of the ring indicated; $(\xi)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \xi\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$
denotes the principal ideal generated by $\xi$.

> **Lemma 3.2.8 (equality criterion for principal local ideals).**
> Let $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{Z}$ and let $r \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $r \geq 0$. Then
> $$p^r(\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \;=\; p^r(\tau - \beta)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$$
> if and only if
> $$v_p\bigl(g(\alpha)\bigr) \;=\; v_p\bigl(g(\beta)\bigr) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \alpha \;\equiv\; \beta \pmod{p^{\,v_p(g(\alpha))}}.$$
>
> Equivalently (since $N(\tau - t) = g(t)$): the two conditions are
> $v_p(N(\tau-\alpha)) = v_p(N(\tau-\beta))$ and
> $\alpha \equiv \beta \bmod p^{\,v_p(N(\tau-\alpha))}$, which is the form in the thesis.

## Role in the development

This is the counting device for Corollary 3.1.3 (the invertible = locally principal ideal
count). Lemma 3.2.4 puts every $\mathfrak{p}\text{-primary}$ ideal of norm $p^m$ in the normal
form $(p^k, p^{m-k}(\tau - A))$; the proof of Corollary 3.1.3 in §3.3 shows that such an ideal
is locally principal exactly when its extension to $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ equals
$p^{m-k}(\tau-\alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ for some integer lift $\alpha$ of $A$. Lemma
3.2.7 and the present lemma then reduce counting the locally principal ideals of norm $p^m$ with
minimal integer $p^k$ to the purely arithmetic problem of counting the residues
$\alpha \bmod p^{2k-m}$ with $v_p(g(\alpha))$ *exactly* $2k-m$; for $k > m/2$ these are the
roots of $g$ modulo $p^{2k-m}$ that do not lift to roots modulo $p^{2k-m+1}$, which Lemma 3.2.6
counts. (For $2k - m \geq 1$ the exact valuation is well defined on residues: $p \mid f$ gives
$v_p(d) \geq 2$, so by $(2\alpha - d)^2 = 4g(\alpha) + d$ every lift of a class containing one
value of valuation $> 2k-m$ has valuation $> 2k-m$. In the boundary stratum $k = m/2$, where
$2k - m = 0$, the single normal-form ideal is $p^{m/2}\mathcal{O}$, whose extension is
$p^{m/2}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = p^{m/2}(\tau - \alpha)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ for any
$\alpha$ with $v_p(g(\alpha)) = 0$; it is locally principal and contributes $1$, not the
non-lifting count.) The proof below uses the normal form and uniqueness of Lemma 3.2.4, the
minimal-integer computation of Lemma 3.2.7, and the identification
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$ of Lemma 3.2.2.

## Proof

Throughout put

$$\nu := v_p\bigl(g(\alpha)\bigr), \qquad \mu := v_p\bigl(g(\beta)\bigr),$$

both finite since $g(\alpha), g(\beta) \neq 0$, and write $g(\alpha) = p^{\nu} u$ with
$u \in \mathbb{Z}$, $p \nmid u$.

**Step 0 (bases and an intersection of subgroups of $\mathbb{Q}$).**
The set $\{1, \tau\}$ is a $\mathbb{Z}\text{-basis}$ of $\mathcal{O}$ and a
$\mathbb{Q}\text{-basis}$ of $K$; for any $t \in \mathbb{Z}$ the unimodular change of basis
$\tau \mapsto \tau - t$ shows $\{1, \tau - t\}$ is again a $\mathbb{Z}\text{-basis}$ of
$\mathcal{O}$ and a $\mathbb{Q}\text{-basis}$ of $K$. Consequently

$$\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}(\tau - t), \qquad \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau] = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}(\tau - t),$$

and every element of $K$ has unique coordinates with respect to $\{1, \tau - t\}$; an element of
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ lies in $\mathcal{O}$ if and only if both of its coordinates lie in
$\mathbb{Z}$. Finally, for every $j \geq 0$,

$$\mathbb{Z} \,\cap\, p^{j}\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} \;=\; p^{j}\mathbb{Z},$$

since an integer of the form $p^{j} a/s$ with $p \nmid s$ has $p\text{-adic}$ valuation
$\geq j$.

**Step 1 ($p\text{-power}$ membership).**

$$p^{\nu} \;\in\; (\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}.$$

Indeed $(\tau - \alpha)(\bar\tau - \alpha) = N(\tau - \alpha) = g(\alpha) = p^{\nu}u$, and
$\bar\tau - \alpha = (d - \alpha) - \tau \in \mathcal{O}$, while $u^{-1} \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$;
hence

$$p^{\nu} \;=\; (\tau - \alpha)\cdot\Bigl(u^{-1}\,(\bar\tau - \alpha)\Bigr) \;\in\; (\tau-\alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}.$$

**Step 2 (contraction in normal form — the identification the thesis delegation hides).** Set

$$k_\alpha := r + \nu, \qquad m_\alpha := 2r + \nu, \qquad \mathfrak{a}_\alpha \;:=\; p^{k_\alpha}\mathbb{Z} \,\oplus\, p^{r}(\tau - \alpha)\mathbb{Z} \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{O}.$$

*Claim.*
$$p^r(\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \,\cap\, \mathcal{O} \;=\; \mathfrak{a}_\alpha,$$
and moreover: $\mathfrak{a}_\alpha$ is an ideal of $\mathcal{O}$ of index
$[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}_\alpha] = p^{m_\alpha}$, its integer elements are exactly
$\mathfrak{a}_\alpha \cap \mathbb{Z} = p^{k_\alpha}\mathbb{Z}$, and $\mathfrak{a}_\alpha$ is
precisely the normal-form ideal $(p^{k}, p^{m-k}(\tau - A))$ of Lemma 3.2.4 with parameters
$$k = k_\alpha, \qquad m = m_\alpha, \qquad [A] = [\alpha] \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{2k-m}, \qquad 2k - m = \nu,$$
for which the root condition $g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k-m}$ holds by the very definition
$\nu = v_p(g(\alpha))$, and $p^{k}$ is the minimal positive integer in the ideal (the conclusion
of Lemma 3.2.7, with $k_\alpha = r + v_p(N(\tau - \alpha))$).

*Proof of the Claim.*

($\supseteq$) Since $r \geq 0$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}$, we have
$p^r(\tau - \alpha) \in \mathcal{O}$, and it lies in $p^r(\tau-\alpha)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$
because $1 \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$. By Step 1,
$p^{k_\alpha} = p^{r}\cdot p^{\nu} \in p^{r}(\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$, and
$p^{k_\alpha} \in \mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$. The left-hand side is an additive subgroup,
so it contains all $\mathbb{Z}\text{-linear}$ combinations of these two elements.

($\subseteq$) Let $\xi \in p^r(\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \cap \mathcal{O}$, say
$\xi = p^r(\tau - \alpha)\gamma$ with $\gamma \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$. By Step 0 write
$\gamma = e_0 + e_1(\tau - \alpha)$ with $e_0, e_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$. From $g(\tau) = 0$,
i.e. $\tau^2 = d\tau - \tfrac{d^2-d}{4}$, one computes

$$(\tau - \alpha)^2 = \tau^2 - 2\alpha\tau + \alpha^2 = (d - 2\alpha)\,(\tau - \alpha) + \bigl[(d-2\alpha)\alpha + \alpha^2 - \tfrac{d^2 - d}{4}\bigr] = (d - 2\alpha)(\tau - \alpha) - g(\alpha).$$

Hence

$$\xi \;=\; p^r\Bigl(e_0(\tau-\alpha) + e_1(\tau-\alpha)^2\Bigr) \;=\; \underbrace{-\,e_1\,u\,p^{\,k_\alpha}}_{=:b_0}\cdot 1 \;+\; \underbrace{p^{r}\bigl(e_0 + e_1(d - 2\alpha)\bigr)}_{=:b_1}\cdot(\tau - \alpha),$$

using $p^r g(\alpha) = u\,p^{r+\nu} = u\,p^{k_\alpha}$. So the coordinates of $\xi$ with respect
to the basis $\{1, \tau - \alpha\}$ are $b_0 \in p^{k_\alpha}\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ and
$b_1 \in p^{r}\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$. Since $\xi \in \mathcal{O}$, Step 0 forces
$b_0, b_1 \in \mathbb{Z}$, and then the valuation intersection of Step 0 gives
$b_0 \in p^{k_\alpha}\mathbb{Z}$ and $b_1 \in p^{r}\mathbb{Z}$. Therefore

$$\xi \;=\; b_0\cdot 1 + b_1\,(\tau - \alpha) \;=\; \frac{b_0}{p^{k_\alpha}}\,p^{k_\alpha} \;+\; \frac{b_1}{p^{r}}\,p^{r}(\tau - \alpha) \;\in\; \mathfrak{a}_\alpha,$$

with both fractions in $\mathbb{Z}$. This proves the displayed equality; the sum defining
$\mathfrak{a}_\alpha$ is direct because $\{1, \tau-\alpha\}$ is a basis.

*Ideal property.* $\mathfrak{a}_\alpha$ is an additive subgroup; stability under multiplication
by $\tau$ (hence by $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z}[\tau]$) follows from the two identities

$$\tau\cdot p^{k_\alpha} \;=\; \alpha\,p^{k_\alpha} \;+\; p^{\nu}\cdot\bigl[p^{r}(\tau - \alpha)\bigr], \qquad \tau\cdot p^{r}(\tau - \alpha) \;=\; -\,u\,p^{k_\alpha} \;+\; (d - \alpha)\cdot\bigl[p^{r}(\tau - \alpha)\bigr],$$

the second using
$\tau(\tau - \alpha) = (\tau-\alpha)^2 + \alpha(\tau - \alpha) = (d - \alpha)(\tau - \alpha) - g(\alpha)$;
all displayed coefficients ($\alpha$, $p^{\nu}$, $-u$, $d - \alpha$) are integers. (This is the
computation of Remark 3.2.5 of the thesis, specialized to the present generators; it is where
$k_\alpha \geq r$, i.e. $\nu \geq 0$, is used.)

*Index.* With respect to the $\mathbb{Z}\text{-basis}$ $\{1, \tau - \alpha\}$ of $\mathcal{O}$,
the sublattice $\mathfrak{a}_\alpha$ is spanned by $p^{k_\alpha}\cdot 1$ and
$p^{r}\cdot(\tau - \alpha)$, a diagonal matrix of determinant
$p^{k_\alpha + r} = p^{2r + \nu} = p^{m_\alpha}$; hence
$[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}_\alpha] = p^{m_\alpha}$.

*Integer elements.* An element $c_1 p^{k_\alpha} + c_2\, p^{r}(\tau - \alpha)$ of
$\mathfrak{a}_\alpha$ ($c_1, c_2 \in \mathbb{Z}$) lies in $\mathbb{Z}$ iff its
$(\tau - \alpha)\text{-coordinate}$ $c_2 p^{r}$ vanishes, iff $c_2 = 0$. So
$\mathfrak{a}_\alpha \cap \mathbb{Z} = p^{k_\alpha}\mathbb{Z}$, and the minimal positive integer
in $\mathfrak{a}_\alpha$ — equivalently, in $p^r(\tau-\alpha)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$, since
positive integers of the local ideal lie in the contraction — is
$p^{k_\alpha} = p^{\,r + v_p(N(\tau-\alpha))}$, which is exactly the statement of Lemma 3.2.7.

*Normal-form position.* Finally
$m_\alpha/2 = r + \nu/2 \leq r + \nu = k_\alpha \leq 2r + \nu = m_\alpha$, and
$m_\alpha - k_\alpha = r$, $2k_\alpha - m_\alpha = \nu$; so
$\mathfrak{a}_\alpha = \bigl(p^{k_\alpha},\, p^{m_\alpha - k_\alpha}(\tau - \alpha)\bigr)$ sits
in the normal form of Lemma 3.2.4 with residue class $[A] = [\alpha] \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{\nu}$ and
with $k_\alpha$ the minimal-integer exponent. This proves the Claim. $\square$

**Step 3 (sufficiency, $\Leftarrow$).** Assume $\nu = \mu$ and
$\alpha \equiv \beta \bmod p^{\nu}$; write $\beta = \alpha + t\,p^{\nu}$ with
$t \in \mathbb{Z}$. By Step 1, $p^{\nu} \in (\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$, hence

$$\tau - \beta \;=\; (\tau - \alpha) - t\,p^{\nu} \;\in\; (\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p},$$

so
$(\tau - \beta)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq (\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$.
Exchanging the roles of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (using $v_p(g(\beta)) = \nu$ and
$\alpha = \beta - t\,p^{\nu}$) gives the reverse inclusion. Multiplying the resulting equality
$(\tau-\alpha)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = (\tau-\beta)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ by $p^{r}$ yields
$p^r(\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = p^r(\tau - \beta)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$.

**Step 4 (necessity, $\Rightarrow$).** Assume
$J := p^r(\tau - \alpha)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = p^r(\tau - \beta)\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$.

*(i) Equality of valuations.* By Lemma 3.2.7 (re-proved concretely in Step 2), the minimal
positive integer contained in $J$ is $p^{\,r + \nu}$, and also $p^{\,r + \mu}$; hence
$r + \nu = r + \mu$, so $\nu = \mu$. If $\nu = 0$ the congruence
$\alpha \equiv \beta \bmod p^{0} = 1$ is vacuous and we are done; assume $\nu \geq 1$ and set
$k := r + \nu$.

*(ii) Congruence of the residues.* Intersecting the equal local ideals with $\mathcal{O}$ and
applying Step 2 to each side:

$$p^{k}\mathbb{Z} \oplus p^{r}(\tau - \alpha)\mathbb{Z} \;=\; J \cap \mathcal{O} \;=\; p^{k}\mathbb{Z} \oplus p^{r}(\tau - \beta)\mathbb{Z}.$$

In particular $p^{r}(\tau - \alpha)$ is a $\mathbb{Z}\text{-linear}$ combination of $p^{k}$ and
$p^{r}(\tau - \beta)$: there are $a_1, a_2 \in \mathbb{Z}$ with

$$p^{r}(\tau - \alpha) \;=\; a_1\,p^{k} + a_2\,p^{r}(\tau - \beta).$$

(In the thesis, the step that passes from $\mathcal{O}\text{-combinations}$ to
$\mathbb{Z}\text{-combinations}$ is Remark 3.2.5, inside the uniqueness argument of Lemma 3.2.4;
here it is immediate because the contraction has been computed as a $\mathbb{Z}\text{-span}$.)
Comparing coordinates with respect to the $\mathbb{Q}\text{-basis}$ $\{1, \tau\}$ of $K$: the
$\tau\text{-coordinate}$ gives $p^{r} = a_2\,p^{r}$, so $a_2 = 1$; the constant coordinate then
gives $-p^{r}\alpha = a_1 p^{k} - p^{r}\beta$, i.e.

$$p^{r}(\beta - \alpha) \;=\; a_1\,p^{k}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \beta - \alpha \;=\; a_1\,p^{\,k - r} \;=\; a_1\,p^{\nu},$$

that is, $\alpha \equiv \beta \pmod{p^{\nu}}$. This is exactly the uniqueness computation of
Lemma 3.2.4 applied to the two normal-form presentations of the common contraction
$J \cap \mathcal{O}$.

$\blacksquare$

## Remarks

**Remark 1 (Divergence from the thesis: standing hypotheses made explicit — ERRATA E6.2).**
The thesis states the lemma as "Given $\alpha, \beta$, and $r \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $r \geq 0$
…", silently inheriting the §3.2 preamble "take $p$ to be a rational prime dividing
$f = \operatorname{cond}(d)$." The hypothesis $p \mid f$ is load-bearing and must appear in any
formal statement: it guarantees a unique prime $\mathfrak{p}$ above $p$ (so the notation
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is unambiguous) and the equality
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$ (Lemma 3.2.2, Remark 3.2.3). For a split
prime the statement read at a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ above $p$ is **false**: take $d = D = -4$ (so
$f = 1$), $\tau = -2 + i$, $g(x) = x^2 + 4x + 5$, $p = 5$ split with $g \equiv x(x-1) \bmod 5$,
and $\mathfrak{p} = (5, \tau)$. Then $\tau - 1 \equiv -1$ and
$\tau - 2 \equiv -2 \bmod \mathfrak{p}$ are both units of $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$, so
$(\tau - 1)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = (\tau - 2)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$,
while $v_5(g(1)) = v_5(10) = 1 \neq 0 = v_5(g(2)) = v_5(17)$: the forward implication fails.
(Lemma 3.2.7 fails at the same example:
$(\tau - 1)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ contains the integer $1$, not
minimal integer $5^{0+1}$.)

**Remark 2 (Scope of the argument; the Lean phrasing).**
Inspecting the proof, the hypothesis $p \mid f$ is used *only* through the identification
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$. Every step of the proof takes place inside
the ring $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau] = \mathcal{O} \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ (the
localization of $\mathcal{O}$ at the multiplicative set $\mathbb{Z} \smallsetminus p\mathbb{Z}$)
and uses only: the basis $\{1,\tau\}$, the identity $N(\tau - t) = g(t) \neq 0$, and
$\mathbb{Z} \cap p^{j}\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = p^{j}\mathbb{Z}$. Consequently the criterion, phrased
for the mapped ideals $p^r(\tau-\alpha)\,\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$, is valid for **every**
rational prime $p$ (split included) with no hypothesis on $f$. The blueprint statement and the
frozen Lean declaration `map_span_tau_sub_eq_iff` nevertheless carry $p \mid f$ and speak of
images of spans under the localization map $\mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ at the
unique prime $\mathfrak{p}$ above $p$, both to match the thesis context and because only under
that hypothesis does the statement say anything about the localization of $\mathcal{O}$ at a
prime ideal. (Remark 1 shows the two readings genuinely diverge when $p$ splits; the
hypothesis-free $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\tau]$ form would be a strictly more general declaration, and
is *not* the frozen one.)

**Remark 3 (What the thesis's two-line proof hides).**
The thesis proof reads, in full: "Note that the intersections with $\mathcal{O}$ are the
$p\text{-primary}$ ideals $(p^{r+v_p(N(\tau-\alpha))}, p^r(\tau-\alpha))$ and
$(p^{r+v_p(N(\tau-\beta))}, p^r(\tau-\beta))$ from Lemma 3.2.7. The result follows from the
uniqueness of the equivalence class of $A$ in Lemma 3.2.4." This delegation hides four
ingredients that a formal proof must supply explicitly:
1. *The contraction identification* (Step 2): the thesis's Lemma 3.2.7 only locates the minimal
   integer of the local ideal; that the full contraction equals the two-generator ideal — indeed
   equals the $\mathbb{Z}\text{-span}$ $p^{k}\mathbb{Z} \oplus p^{r}(\tau - A)\mathbb{Z}$ in
   normal-form position for Lemma 3.2.4 — is a separate computation, carried out above. (For
   this reason the blueprint's Lemma 3.2.7 node is *strengthened* to state the contraction
   identity itself; Step 2 re-proves it here to keep this document self-contained.)
2. *Matching the parameter* $k$: Lemma 3.2.4's uniqueness compares two presentations with the
   *same* $k$; that the two contractions share $k$ is Step 4(i), i.e. Lemma 3.2.7 applied twice.
3. *Primarity is not free*: Lemma 3.2.4's uniqueness clause is stated for
   $\mathfrak{p}\text{-primary}$ ideals of norm $p^m$; the proof above bypasses any need to
   verify primarity of the contraction by computing it as a $\mathbb{Z}\text{-span}$, after
   which the uniqueness argument is an elementary coordinate comparison. This also handles the
   degenerate corner $r = \nu = 0$, where the contraction is all of $\mathcal{O}$ — which is
   *not* a primary ideal, so the thesis's citation of 3.2.4 does not literally apply there.
4. *The converse direction*: the "if" half (Step 3) is not contained in Lemmas 3.2.4/3.2.7 at
   all; it needs the membership $p^{\nu} \in (\tau - \alpha)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ from Step 1.

**Remark 4 (The exponent $r$ is inert, and is forced to match).**
Since $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is a domain and $p^r \neq 0$, one has
$p^r\xi\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = p^r\eta\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ iff
$\xi\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = \eta\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$; the criterion is really about
$(\tau - \alpha)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ vs. $(\tau - \beta)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$, and the
factor $p^r$ is carried only because Corollary 3.1.3's proof produces generators in the shape
$p^{m-k}(\tau - \alpha)$. Moreover distinct exponents can never give equal ideals of this shape:
if $p^{r}(\tau - \alpha)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p} = p^{r'}(\tau - \beta)\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$
then comparing minimal integers gives $r + \nu = r' + \mu$ and comparing contraction indices
(Step 2) gives $2r + \nu = 2r' + \mu$; subtracting, $r = r'$ (and then $\nu = \mu$).

**Remark 5 (Norm versus polynomial value; sign conventions).**
The thesis states the criterion with $v_p(N(\tau - \alpha))$; this document (and the Lean
statement) uses $v_p(g(\alpha))$, and the two agree by the identity $N(\tau - t) = g(t)$ proved
in the Setting. Note also that the hypothesis $d < 0$ is used only to ensure $g(t) \neq 0$ for
all $t \in \mathbb{Z}$; any non-square $d$ would do. The congruence condition is stated with
modulus $p^{v_p(g(\alpha))}$, formally asymmetric in $\alpha, \beta$; in the presence of the
first condition $v_p(g(\alpha)) = v_p(g(\beta))$ it is symmetric. The two conditions are
independent: $\alpha \equiv \beta \bmod p^{v_p(g(\alpha))}$ alone gives only
$v_p(g(\beta)) \geq v_p(g(\alpha))$ (from
$g(\beta) - g(\alpha) = (\beta - \alpha)(\beta + \alpha - d)$), not equality.
