# Theorem 3.1.2* (CORRECTED): all ideals of norm $p^m$ in $\mathcal{O}_d$, $p \mid f$

This document states and proves the **corrected, $p$-uniform** form of Theorem 3.1.2 of
the thesis (*Singular Moduli and the Ideal Class Group*, C. Geiger, UW 2020), as mandated
by ERRATA E1, E2, E4. The thesis's separate five-case $2$-adic display is **not**
reproduced here: two of its five branches are false as stated (E1, E2), and after
correction the entire $p = 2$ case coincides with the odd-$p$ formulas evaluated at
$p = 2$ (E4). See the Remarks for a precise list of divergences.

## Setting and notation

All notation is restated here; the reader does not need the thesis open.

* $D < 0$ is a **fundamental discriminant**: either $D \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $D$ is
  squarefree, or $D = 4D_0$ with $D_0 \equiv 2, 3 \pmod 4$ squarefree. In particular,
  for an odd prime $p \mid D$ we have $v_p(D) = 1$, and $v_2(D) \in \{0, 2, 3\}$ with
  * $v_2(D) = 0 \iff D \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ (and then $D \equiv 1$ or $5 \pmod 8$),
  * $v_2(D) = 2 \iff D \equiv 4 \pmod 8$, in which case $D/4 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$,
  * $v_2(D) = 3 \iff D \equiv 8 \pmod{16}$.
* $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)$ is the associated imaginary quadratic field with maximal
  order $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}\big[\tfrac{D + \sqrt D}{2}\big]$.
* $f \geq 1$ is an integer (the **conductor**) and $d := D f^2$, so $d$ is a (negative)
  discriminant, $d \equiv 0, 1 \pmod 4$.
* $\mathcal{O}_d := \mathbb{Z}[\tau]$ with $\tau := \tfrac{d + \sqrt d}{2}$ is the order
  of discriminant $d$ and conductor $f$ in $K$ (note $\sqrt d = f \sqrt D$, so
  $\mathcal{O}_d \subseteq \mathcal{O}_K$ with index $f$). The minimal polynomial of
  $\tau$ is
  $$g(x) := x^2 - d\,x + \frac{d^2 - d}{4} \in \mathbb{Z}[x],$$
  so $\mathcal{O}_d \cong \mathbb{Z}[x]/(g)$, and $\{1, \tau\}$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis
  of $\mathcal{O}_d$.
* The **norm** of a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak a \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d$ is its index as
  a sublattice, $N(\mathfrak a) := [\mathcal{O}_d : \mathfrak a] =
  \#(\mathcal{O}_d / \mathfrak a)$, which is finite.
* $p$ is a rational prime with $p \mid f$, and $w := v_p(f) \geq 1$.
* $m \geq 0$ is an integer; $\varepsilon := 1$ if $m$ is even and $\varepsilon := 0$ if
  $m$ is odd (the thesis's convention — note it is the *reverse* of the natural parity
  indicator, cf. ERRATA E6.6); $\mu := m \bmod 2 = 1 - \varepsilon$.
* $v := v_p(d/4)$, with the ERRATA E6.8 convention:
  $$v := \begin{cases} v_p(d) & p \text{ odd (here } d/4 \text{ need not be an integer)},\\
  v_2(d) - 2 & p = 2 \text{ (legitimate: } 2 \mid f \Rightarrow v_2(d) = v_2(D) + 2w \geq 2\text{)}. \end{cases}$$
  Since $d = D f^2$,
  $$v = \begin{cases} v_p(D) + 2w & p \text{ odd}, \\ v_2(D) + 2w - 2 & p = 2. \end{cases}$$
* Splitting behavior of $p$ in $\mathcal{O}_K$ (equivalently, the Kronecker symbol
  $\big(\tfrac{D}{p}\big)$): $p$ is **ramified** iff $p \mid D$; for $p$ odd, $p$ is
  **split**/**inert** iff the Legendre symbol $\big(\tfrac{D}{p}\big) = +1$/$-1$; for
  $p = 2$, split iff $D \equiv 1 \pmod 8$, inert iff $D \equiv 5 \pmod 8$.

Throughout, "ideal" means a nonzero integral ideal of $\mathcal{O}_d$; the count below
includes **non-invertible** ideals (the invertible count is Corollary 3.1.3*, a separate
document).

## Statement

> **Theorem 3.1.2\* ($p$-uniform corrected form).** With the notation above (in
> particular $p \mid f$, $w = v_p(f)$, $v = v_p(d/4)$, $\varepsilon = 1$ iff $m$ even),
> the number of ideals of norm $p^m$ in $\mathcal{O}_d$ is:
>
> * if $v \geq m$:
>   $$\#\{\mathfrak a \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d : [\mathcal{O}_d : \mathfrak a] = p^m\}
>   \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m/2 \rfloor} p^i \;=\; \frac{p^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor + 1} - 1}{p - 1};$$
> * if $v < m$:
>   $$\#\{\mathfrak a \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d : [\mathcal{O}_d : \mathfrak a] = p^m\}
>   \;=\; \begin{cases}
>   \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{w} p^i = \frac{p^{w+1} - 1}{p-1}
>     & p \text{ ramified in } \mathcal{O}_K \text{ (i.e. } p \mid D\text{)},\\[2ex]
>   \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{w+\varepsilon-1} p^i = \frac{p^{w+\varepsilon} - 1}{p-1}
>     & p \text{ inert in } \mathcal{O}_K,\\[2ex]
>   \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{w+\varepsilon-1} p^i \;+\; p^w\,(m + 1 - 2w - \varepsilon)
>     & p \text{ split in } \mathcal{O}_K.
>   \end{cases}$$
>
> The statement is uniform in $p$: there is **no** separate display for $p = 2$.

(In the split case with $v < m$ one always has $m + 1 - 2w - \varepsilon \geq 0$; see
Step 5, subcases (b2) and (b4), of the proof.)

## Role in the development

This is the headline counting theorem of Chapter 3: it answers "how many ideals of
$\mathcal{O}_d$ have norm $p^m$" for the difficult primes $p \mid f$, where unique
factorization into primes fails (the case $p \nmid f$ reduces to the maximal-order count,
Proposition 3.1.1). Its proof consumes the normal-form bijection
Lemma 3.2.4 ([lem-3-2-4]) and the root-counting Lemma 3.2.6 ([lem-3-2-6]), together with
the index/norm infrastructure ([infra-index]) and the standing setup ([notation]).
Downstream, Corollary 3.1.3* (the invertible-ideal count) refines this theorem via the
localization Lemmas 3.2.7/3.2.8, and a CRT gluing step (not in the thesis) will combine
it with Proposition 3.1.1 into a count for arbitrary norm $n$ — the quantity entering the
Lauter–Viray $A(N)$ analysis that motivates the whole chapter.

## Proof

### Ingredients

We restate the two external lemmas in the exact form used.

> **Lemma A (normal form; [lem-3-2-4], corrected per ERRATA E6.4).** Let $p \mid f$ and
> $m \geq 0$. The assignment
> $$(k, [A]) \;\longmapsto\; \mathfrak a_{k,A} := p^k\,\mathbb{Z} \,\oplus\,
> p^{m-k}(\tau - A)\,\mathbb{Z}$$
> is a bijection from
> $$\big\{(k, [A]) : k \in \mathbb{Z},\ \lceil m/2 \rceil \leq k \leq m,\
> [A] \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{2k-m},\ g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k-m}\big\}$$
> onto $\{\mathfrak a \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d \text{ ideal} :
> [\mathcal{O}_d : \mathfrak a] = p^m\}$. Moreover $\mathfrak a_{k,A} \cap \mathbb{Z} =
> p^k \mathbb{Z}$, so $k$ (and then $[A]$) is determined by the ideal: $p^k$ is the
> minimal power of $p$ contained in it.

(The condition $g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k-m}$ is well defined on the class
$[A] \bmod p^{2k-m}$. For $2k - m = 0$, i.e. $m$ even and $k = m/2$, the convention is
that $\mathbb{Z}/p^0$ is the zero ring with its single, vacuously admissible class.
Lemma A already incorporates the two "bridge" facts of ERRATA E6.4: (a) since $p \mid f$
there is a unique prime of $\mathcal{O}_d$ above $p$ — because $g \bmod p$ is the square
of a linear polynomial, so $\mathcal{O}_d/p\mathcal{O}_d \cong
\mathbb{F}_p[x]/((x - A_0)^2)$ is local — hence every ideal of $p$-power index is
$p$-primary and Lemma 3.2.4's normal form applies to *all* ideals of index $p^m$; and
(b) the minimal-integer statement $\mathfrak a_{k,A} \cap \mathbb{Z} = p^k\mathbb{Z}$,
which rules out double counting across different $k$.)

> **Lemma B (roots of $x^2 = p^{2r}u$; [lem-3-2-6], corrected per ERRATA E6.5).** Let
> $p$ be a prime, $r, s$ integers with $0 \leq 2r < s$, and $u$ an integer coprime to
> $p$. Then
> $$\#\{x \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s : x^2 \equiv p^{2r} u \bmod p^s\} \;=\;
> \begin{cases}
> 2p^r & p \text{ odd and } \big(\tfrac{u}{p}\big) = 1,\\
> 0 & p \text{ odd and } \big(\tfrac{u}{p}\big) = -1,\\
> 2^{r} & p = 2,\ s - 2r = 1 \text{ (unconditionally)},\\
> 2^{r+1} & p = 2,\ s - 2r = 2,\ u \equiv 1 \bmod 4,\\
> 2^{r+2} & p = 2,\ s - 2r \geq 3,\ u \equiv 1 \bmod 8,\\
> 0 & p = 2 \text{ otherwise}.
> \end{cases}$$
> The count depends on $u$ only through $u \bmod p^{\min(s-2r,\,3)}$ (for $p = 2$)
> resp. $u \bmod p$ (for $p$ odd, where "$u$ is a QR mod $p^{s-2r}$" is equivalent to
> $\big(\tfrac{u}{p}\big) = 1$ by Hensel lifting).

**Caution (this is where the thesis proof broke):** the case distinction in Lemma B is
governed by $s - 2r$, **not** by $s$. Tracking this correctly through the summation
below is the entire content of the corrections E1–E3.

We also isolate a trivial count.

> **Lemma C (degenerate case).** For any $s \geq 0$,
> $\#\{x \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s : x^2 \equiv 0 \bmod p^s\} = p^{\lfloor s/2 \rfloor}$.

*Proof of Lemma C.* $x^2 \equiv 0 \bmod p^s$ iff $2\,v_p(x) \geq s$ iff
$x \in p^{\lceil s/2 \rceil}\mathbb{Z}/p^s\mathbb{Z}$, a set of size
$p^{s - \lceil s/2 \rceil} = p^{\lfloor s/2 \rfloor}$. $\blacksquare$

### Step 1: reduction to root counting

Write $r(p^m) := \#\{\mathfrak a \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d :
[\mathcal{O}_d : \mathfrak a] = p^m\}$. By Lemma A, grouping ideals by their minimal
power $p^k$,
$$r(p^m) \;=\; \sum_{k = \lceil m/2 \rceil}^{m} N_k, \qquad
N_k := \#\{A \in \mathbb{Z}/p^{2k-m} : g(A) \equiv 0 \bmod p^{2k-m}\}.$$
For the rest of the proof set $s = s(k) := 2k - m$. As $k$ runs over
$\lceil m/2 \rceil \leq k \leq m$, $s$ runs over the integers of the same parity as $m$
in the range $\mu \leq s \leq m$ ($\mu = m \bmod 2$), and
$$k - \lceil m/2 \rceil = \frac{s - \mu}{2} = \Big\lfloor \frac{s}{2} \Big\rfloor .
\tag{1.1}$$

### Step 2: completing the square

In $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12][x]$ one has the exact identity
$$g(x) = x^2 - dx + \frac{d^2 - d}{4} = \Big(x - \frac d2\Big)^2 - \frac d4. \tag{2.1}$$

*Case $p$ odd.* $2$ is invertible in $\mathbb{Z}/p^s$; let $\bar 2, \bar 4$ denote
inverses of $2, 4$. The affine substitution $x = y + d\,\bar 2$ is a bijection of
$\mathbb{Z}/p^s$, and by (2.1), $g(y + d\,\bar 2) \equiv y^2 - d\,\bar 4 \bmod p^s$.

*Case $p = 2$.* Since $2 \mid f$, $v_2(d) \geq 2$, so $d/2, d/4 \in \mathbb{Z}$ and
(2.1) holds in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$; the substitution $x = y + d/2$ is a bijection of
$\mathbb{Z}/2^s$.

In both cases, setting $q := d\,\bar 4 \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s$ (odd $p$) resp.
$q := d/4 \in \mathbb{Z}$ ($p = 2$),
$$N_k = \#\{y \in \mathbb{Z}/p^s : y^2 \equiv q \bmod p^s\},$$
and the valuation of $q$ is controlled by $v$: if $v < s$ then every integer lift
$\tilde q$ of $q$ has $v_p(\tilde q) = v$ exactly, while if $v \geq s$ then
$q \equiv 0 \bmod p^s$. (For odd $p$ this is because $\bar 4$ is a unit and
$v = v_p(d)$; for $p = 2$ because $v = v_2(d/4)$ by definition.)

### Step 3: the valuation dichotomy; the case $v \geq m$

Set $\kappa := \tfrac{v + m}{2} \in \tfrac12\mathbb{Z}$. Then
$$k \leq \kappa \iff s \leq v, \qquad k > \kappa \iff s > v.$$

**Sub-$\kappa$ regime.** If $s \leq v$ then $q \equiv 0 \bmod p^s$, so by Lemma C and
(1.1),
$$N_k = p^{\lfloor s/2 \rfloor} = p^{\,k - \lceil m/2 \rceil}. \tag{3.1}$$

**Case $v \geq m$ (first branch of the theorem).** Every $k$ in range has
$s \leq m \leq v$, so by (3.1),
$$r(p^m) = \sum_{k=\lceil m/2\rceil}^{m} p^{\,k - \lceil m/2 \rceil}
= \sum_{i=0}^{m - \lceil m/2 \rceil} p^i = \sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m/2 \rfloor} p^i,$$
as claimed. (In particular $m = 0$ always lands here, giving the correct count $1$ for
the unit ideal.) This proves the first branch; **from now on assume $v < m$.**

### Step 4: the sub-$\kappa$ geometric block $\Sigma_0$

Let
$$\Sigma_0 := \sum_{\lceil m/2 \rceil \le k \le \kappa} p^{\,k - \lceil m/2\rceil}
= \sum_{i=0}^{i_0} p^i, \qquad
i_0 := \lfloor \kappa \rfloor - \lceil m/2 \rceil,$$
with the convention that the sum is empty (equal to $0$) if $i_0 < 0$. Using
$\lfloor \kappa \rfloor = \lfloor (v+m)/2 \rfloor$ and
$\lceil m/2 \rceil = (m + \mu)/2$:

* if $v$ is odd, $\lfloor (v+m)/2 \rfloor = \tfrac{v-1}{2} + \lceil m/2 \rceil$, so
  $i_0 = \tfrac{v-1}{2}$ (independent of the parity of $m$);
* if $v$ is even, $\lfloor (v+m)/2 \rfloor = \tfrac v2 + \lfloor m/2 \rfloor$, so
  $i_0 = \tfrac v2 - \mu = \tfrac v2 - 1 + \varepsilon$.

### Step 5: the super-$\kappa$ regime, case by case

Now consider $k$ with $\kappa < k \leq m$, i.e.
$$s \in S := \{s \in \mathbb{Z} : v < s \leq m,\ s \equiv m \bmod 2\}.$$
$S \neq \emptyset$: its least element $s_1$ is $v + 1$ if $v + 1 \equiv m \bmod 2$ and
$v + 2$ otherwise, and $s_1 \leq m$ in both cases (if $s_1 = v+2$ then $v \equiv m
\bmod 2$ and $v < m$ force $m \geq v + 2$).

Recall $v = v_p(D) + 2w$ ($p$ odd) and $v = v_2(D) + 2w - 2$ ($p = 2$). We distinguish
by the parity of $v$ and, when $v$ is even, apply Lemma B.

**(a) $v$ odd** — this happens exactly when $p$ is odd and $p \mid D$ (then
$v_p(D) = 1$, $v = 2w + 1$), or $p = 2$ and $D \equiv 8 \bmod 16$ (then $v_2(D) = 3$,
$v = 2w + 1$); both are ramified cases. *Claim: $N_k = 0$ for every $s \in S$.* Indeed,
suppose $y \in \mathbb{Z}$ satisfies $y^2 \equiv q \bmod p^s$ with $s > v$, and let
$\tilde q$ be an integer lift of $q$, so $v_p(\tilde q) = v$ (Step 2). Then
$v_p(y^2 - \tilde q) \geq s > v$. If $v_p(y^2) \neq v$, the ultrametric inequality gives
$v_p(y^2 - \tilde q) = \min(v_p(y^2), v) \leq v < s$, a contradiction; so
$v_p(y^2) = v$, which is impossible because $v_p(y^2) = 2 v_p(y)$ is even while $v$ is
odd. Hence, using Step 4 with $v = 2w + 1$:
$$r(p^m) = \Sigma_0 = \sum_{i=0}^{w} p^i,$$
which is the ramified formula.

**(b) $v$ even.** Write $r := v/2$ and pick an integer $u$, coprime to $p$, with
$q \equiv p^{2r} u \bmod p^s$ (possible since $s > v$; concretely):

* $p$ odd, $p \nmid D$ (inert or split): $v = 2w$, $r = w$, and
  $u \equiv (d/p^{2w})\,\bar 4 \equiv D\,(f/p^w)^2\,\bar 4 \bmod p$, so
  $\big(\tfrac up\big) = \big(\tfrac Dp\big)$ (as $(f/p^w)^2$ and $\bar 4$ are nonzero
  squares mod $p$);
* $p = 2$, $D$ odd (inert or split): $v = 2w - 2$, $r = w - 1$, and writing
  $f = 2^w f'$ with $f'$ odd, $d/4 = D f'^2\, 2^{2w-2}$, so $u = D f'^2 \equiv D \bmod
  8$ (odd squares are $\equiv 1 \bmod 8$); in particular $u \equiv 1 \bmod 4$ always
  (as $D \equiv 1 \bmod 4$), and $u \equiv 1 \bmod 8$ iff $D \equiv 1 \bmod 8$ iff $2$
  splits;
* $p = 2$, $D \equiv 4 \bmod 8$ (ramified): $v = 2w$, $r = w$, and
  $d/4 = (D/4) f'^2\, 2^{2w}$, so $u = (D/4) f'^2 \equiv D/4 \equiv 3 \bmod 4$.

Since $2r = v < s$ for all $s \in S$, Lemma B applies with this $r$ and $u$; note
$s - 2r = s - v$.

**(b1) $p$ odd, inert** ($\big(\tfrac Dp\big) = -1$): $N_k = 0$ for all $s \in S$, so
with Step 4 ($v = 2w$ even):
$$r(p^m) = \Sigma_0 = \sum_{i=0}^{w - 1 + \varepsilon} p^i
= \sum_{i=0}^{w + \varepsilon - 1} p^i,$$
the inert formula.

**(b2) $p$ odd, split** ($\big(\tfrac Dp\big) = 1$): $N_k = 2p^w$ for *every*
$s \in S$. Count $|S|$: if $m$ is even, $S = \{2w+2, 2w+4, \dots, m\}$ has
$\tfrac{m - 2w}{2}$ elements; if $m$ is odd, $S = \{2w+1, 2w+3, \dots, m\}$ has
$\tfrac{m + 1 - 2w}{2}$ elements; uniformly $|S| = \tfrac{m + 1 - 2w - \varepsilon}{2}
\geq 1$. Hence
$$r(p^m) = \Sigma_0 + 2p^w \cdot \frac{m + 1 - 2w - \varepsilon}{2}
= \sum_{i=0}^{w+\varepsilon-1} p^i + p^w\,(m + 1 - 2w - \varepsilon),$$
the split formula.

**(b3) $p = 2$, $D \equiv 4 \bmod 8$** (ramified, $v = 2w$, $r = w$,
$u \equiv 3 \bmod 4$): by Lemma B, a value $s \in S$ contributes iff $s - 2r = 1$,
i.e. $s = 2w + 1$; this $s$ lies in $S$ exactly when $m$ is odd (and then indeed
$2w + 1 \leq m$ since $v = 2w < m$), and contributes $N_k = 2^{r} = 2^w$. The cases
$s - 2r = 2$ (needs $u \equiv 1 \bmod 4$) and $s - 2r \geq 3$ (needs
$u \equiv 1 \bmod 8$) are excluded by $u \equiv 3 \bmod 4$. Hence
$$r(2^m) = \Sigma_0 + (1 - \varepsilon)\,2^w
= \sum_{i=0}^{w-1+\varepsilon} 2^i + (1-\varepsilon)\,2^w
= \sum_{i=0}^{w} 2^i \qquad (\text{both parities of } m),$$
where the last equality is checked separately: $m$ even gives
$\sum_{i=0}^{w} 2^i + 0$; $m$ odd gives $(2^w - 1) + 2^w = 2^{w+1} - 1$. This is the
ramified formula. *(This is correction E2: the thesis claimed $2^{w+\varepsilon} - 1$,
which is wrong for $m$ odd.)*

**(b4) $p = 2$, $D$ odd** ($v = 2w - 2$, $r = w - 1$, $u \equiv D \bmod 8$). Here
$s - 2r = s - (2w - 2)$, and by Lemma B:

* $s = 2w - 1$ (i.e. $s - 2r = 1$; lies in $S$ iff $m$ odd, and then $2w - 1 \le m$
  since $m > v = 2w-2$): contributes $2^{w-1}$, unconditionally;
* $s = 2w$ (i.e. $s - 2r = 2$; lies in $S$ iff $m$ even, and then $2w \le m$):
  contributes $2^{w}$, since $u \equiv 1 \bmod 4$ holds ($D \equiv 1 \bmod 4$);
* $s \geq 2w + 1$ (i.e. $s - 2r \geq 3$): contributes $2^{w+1}$ if
  $D \equiv 1 \bmod 8$ ($2$ split), and $0$ if $D \equiv 5 \bmod 8$ ($2$ inert).

So the least element $s_1$ of $S$ ($= 2w-1$ or $2w$ according to parity, i.e. the
$k = \lfloor\kappa\rfloor + 1$ term) contributes exactly
$$2^{\,w - 1 + \varepsilon} \quad\text{for \emph{every} } w \geq 1. \tag{5.1}$$
*(This is the crux of corrections E1/E3: the thesis tested "$2k - m \in \{1,2\}$", i.e.
$s \in \{1, 2\}$, instead of $s - 2r \in \{1, 2\}$, and so found this boundary term only
when $w = 1$.)*

**Inert** ($D \equiv 5 \bmod 8$): only $s_1$ contributes beyond $\kappa$, so with
Step 4 ($v = 2w - 2$, $i_0 = w - 2 + \varepsilon$):
$$r(2^m) = \Big(\sum_{i=0}^{w-2+\varepsilon} 2^i\Big) + 2^{w-1+\varepsilon}
= \big(2^{w-1+\varepsilon} - 1\big) + 2^{w-1+\varepsilon}
= 2^{w+\varepsilon} - 1 = \sum_{i=0}^{w+\varepsilon-1} 2^i,$$
the inert formula. (When $w = 1$ and $m$ is odd, $\Sigma_0$ is empty and the formula
still holds: $0 + 2^0 = 1 = 2^{1+0} - 1$.) *(This is correction E1.)*

**Split** ($D \equiv 1 \bmod 8$): beyond $s_1$, each $s \in S$ with $s \geq 2w + 1$
contributes $2^{w+1}$; as in (b2) the number of such $s$ is
$\tfrac{m + 1 - 2w - \varepsilon}{2} \geq 0$ (possibly $0$, when $m = 2w - 1$ or
$m = 2w$). Hence
$$r(2^m) = \big(2^{w-1+\varepsilon} - 1\big) + 2^{w-1+\varepsilon}
+ 2^{w+1}\cdot\frac{m+1-2w-\varepsilon}{2}
= \sum_{i=0}^{w+\varepsilon-1} 2^i + 2^w\,(m + 1 - 2w - \varepsilon),$$
the split formula.

### Step 6: assembly

Every case with $v < m$ has been covered — (a) and (b3) together are exactly "$p$
ramified" ($p \mid D$), (b1) and the inert half of (b4) are "$p$ inert", (b2) and the
split half of (b4) are "$p$ split" — and each total matches the claimed $p$-uniform
display. Summary ($v < m$; "boundary"/"tail" are the super-$\kappa$ contributions):

| case | $v$ | $r$ | $\Sigma_0$ | boundary term | tail terms | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $p$ odd ram., or $p=2$, $D \equiv 8\,(16)$ | $2w+1$ | — | $\sum_0^{w} p^i$ | — | — | $\sum_{i=0}^{w} p^i$ |
| $p = 2$, $D \equiv 4\,(8)$ | $2w$ | $w$ | $\sum_0^{w-1+\varepsilon} 2^i$ | $(1-\varepsilon)\,2^w$ at $s = 2w{+}1$ | — | $\sum_{i=0}^{w} 2^i$ |
| $p$ odd inert | $2w$ | $w$ | $\sum_0^{w-1+\varepsilon} p^i$ | — | — | $\sum_{i=0}^{w+\varepsilon-1} p^i$ |
| $p = 2$ inert | $2w{-}2$ | $w{-}1$ | $\sum_0^{w-2+\varepsilon} 2^i$ | $2^{w-1+\varepsilon}$ at $s_1$ | — | $\sum_{i=0}^{w+\varepsilon-1} 2^i$ |
| $p$ odd split | $2w$ | $w$ | $\sum_0^{w-1+\varepsilon} p^i$ | — | $2p^w$ each, $\frac{m+1-2w-\varepsilon}{2}$ values | $\sum_{i=0}^{w+\varepsilon-1} p^i + p^w(m{+}1{-}2w{-}\varepsilon)$ |
| $p = 2$ split | $2w{-}2$ | $w{-}1$ | $\sum_0^{w-2+\varepsilon} 2^i$ | $2^{w-1+\varepsilon}$ at $s_1$ | $2^{w+1}$ each, $\frac{m+1-2w-\varepsilon}{2}$ values | $\sum_{i=0}^{w+\varepsilon-1} 2^i + 2^w(m{+}1{-}2w{-}\varepsilon)$ |

$\blacksquare$

## Remarks

### Remark 1 — Divergence from the thesis (E1, E2, E4)

The thesis states Theorem 3.1.2 in two displays: the odd-$p$ display (hypothesis
"$v_p(d/4) < m$ and $p \neq 2$") with the three case formulas above, and a separate
five-case $2$-adic display. This document diverges from the thesis exactly as follows:

1. **E1 (inert $2$-adic branch, $w \geq 2$, is false).** The thesis claims
   $2^{w-1+\varepsilon} - 1$ ideals when $2$ is inert and $w \neq 1$ (with a special
   value $2^{1+\varepsilon} - 1$ at $w = 1$). The correct count is
   $2^{w+\varepsilon} - 1$ for **all** $w \geq 1$; the thesis's $w = 1$ special value
   agrees with this, the $w \neq 1$ branch does not. Counterexamples: see the table in
   Remark 3.
2. **E2 (ramified $2$-adic branch with $D \equiv 4 \bmod 8$, $m$ odd, is false).** The
   thesis claims $2^{w+\varepsilon} - 1$ (which equals $2^w - 1$ for odd $m$). The
   correct count is $2^{w+1} - 1$ for **both** parities of $m$. (The thesis is
   internally inconsistent here: its own proof of Corollary 3.1.3's ramified case
   produces $2^w$ *invertible* ideals of norm $2^m$ in this situation, exceeding the
   claimed total $2^w - 1$.)
3. **E4 (the separate $2$-adic display is dissolved).** After the corrections, all
   five $2$-adic branches coincide with the odd-$p$ formulas evaluated at $p = 2$
   ($D \equiv 0 \bmod 8$ and $D \equiv 4 \bmod 8$ merge into "ramified":
   $2^{w+1} - 1$; inert: $2^{w+\varepsilon} - 1$; split:
   $2^{w+\varepsilon} - 1 + 2^w(m + 1 - 2w - \varepsilon)$). Accordingly, the statement
   here is a single $p$-uniform theorem, and the hypothesis "$p \neq 2$" on the first
   display is dropped. The thesis's $D \equiv 0 \bmod 8$ and split $2$-adic branches
   were already correct and are unchanged in content.

The **first branch** ($v \geq m$) and the odd-$p$ display are stated as in the thesis
(only the $\sum p^i$ form is added alongside the closed form).

### Remark 2 — Root cause of E1/E2 (E3)

In Step 5(b4), solvability of $y^2 \equiv 2^{2r}u \bmod 2^s$ is governed by
$s - 2r = (2k - m) - 2(w - 1)$, whereas the thesis's §3.3 proof tested
$2k - m \in \{1, 2\}$ — i.e. it implicitly took $r = 0$. Consequently the thesis found
the boundary contribution (5.1) only when $w = 1$ (where $s_1 = 2w - 1 = 1$ or
$s_1 = 2w = 2$), and encoded it as a correction term $\delta \cdot 2^{\varepsilon}$ with
$\delta = [w = 1]$. The correct boundary term $2^{w-1+\varepsilon}$ is present for
*every* $w$ whenever $v < m$, and equals the thesis's term exactly at $w = 1$. The same
oversight, in the $D \equiv 4 \bmod 8$ case (Step 5(b3), $r = w$, boundary at
$s = 2w + 1$), produces E2.

### Remark 4 — Conventions and pitfalls

* **$\varepsilon$ is the reverse of the natural parity flag** ($\varepsilon = 1$ iff
  $m$ *even*; ERRATA E6.6). The Lean statement should prefer `Even m` case splits or
  `ε := 1 - m % 2`.
* **$v_p(d/4)$** (ERRATA E6.8) is *notation*, not a literal valuation of a rational:
  it means $v_p(d)$ for odd $p$ (where $d/4$ need not be an integer) and
  $v_2(d) - 2$ for $p = 2$ (where $2 \mid f$ forces $4 \mid d$). Concretely
  $v = v_p(D) + 2w$ ($p$ odd), $v = v_2(D) + 2w - 2$ ($p = 2$).
* **Boundary convention.** This theorem splits its regimes at $v \geq m$ vs. $v < m$.
  Corollary 3.1.3* (invertible count) uses a *different* and subtler regime boundary;
  per ERRATA E5 the two must **not** be harmonized — each is forced as stated.
* The count includes non-invertible ideals; $\#\{\mathfrak a : [\mathcal{O} :
  \mathfrak a] = p^m\}$ here and the typographically identical LHS of Corollary 3.1.3
  in the thesis denote *different* quantities (ERRATA E6.1).
* $m = 0$ is covered by the $v \geq m$ branch (count $1$); no separate hypothesis
  $m > 0$ is needed for this theorem (contrast Corollary 3.1.3*, ERRATA E5).
* Degenerate split regime: for $p = 2$ split with $m = 2w - 1$ or $m = 2w$ (the least
  $m$ allowed by $v < m$), the split tail $p^w(m + 1 - 2w - \varepsilon)$ vanishes and
  the split and inert counts coincide. This is correct, not an error.
