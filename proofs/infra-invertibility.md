# infra-invertibility — Invertibility interface: invertible = locally principal (infrastructure)

## Statement

**Setting and notation.** Throughout, $d$ is a negative integer with $d \equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod 4$
(an *imaginary quadratic discriminant*). Write $d = D f^2$ where $D$ is the fundamental
discriminant of $K := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt d)$ and $f = \operatorname{cond}(d) \ge 1$ is the
*conductor*; for a prime $p \mid f$ we write $w := v_p(f) \ge 1$ (not needed below, but fixed
here because every document in this development uses it). Fix the square root
$\sqrt d := f\sqrt D$. The maximal order is $O_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ with
$\omega := \tfrac{D+\sqrt D}{2}$, and

$$\mathcal{O} = \mathcal{O}_d := \mathbb{Z}[\tau], \qquad \tau := \frac{d+\sqrt d}{2} = \frac{Df^2 + f\sqrt D}{2},$$

is the unique order of discriminant $d$ in $K$: a free $\mathbb{Z}\text{-module}$ with basis
$1, \tau$, where $\tau$ has minimal polynomial $g(x) = x^2 - dx + \tfrac{d^2-d}{4}$. Conjugation
$\alpha \mapsto \bar\alpha$ is the nontrivial automorphism of $K$; it preserves $\mathcal{O}$
(since $\bar\tau = d - \tau$), and $N(\alpha) := \alpha\bar\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}$ for
$\alpha \in \mathcal{O}$. For a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$, the *index*
$[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] := \#(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a})$ is finite (doc *infra-index*) and
is the ideal norm used throughout the development.

For an integral domain $R$ with fraction field $F$ and a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq R$,
the localization is realized inside $F$:
$$R_\mathfrak{p} := \left\{ \tfrac{a}{s} : a \in R,\ s \in R \smallsetminus \mathfrak{p} \right\} \subseteq F,$$
and for an ideal $I \subseteq R$ we write $I R_\mathfrak{p}$ for the ideal of $R_\mathfrak{p}$
generated by $I$. An ideal is *principal* if it is generated by one element (the zero ideal and
the unit ideal are principal). A maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $\mathcal{O}_d$ has *residue
characteristic* $q$ when $\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z} = q\mathbb{Z}$; we also say
$\mathfrak{m}$ *lies over* $q$.

This result is **not in the thesis**; the thesis uses the equivalence silently (see the
Remarks). The definition below is the one frozen for the Lean development.

> **Definition 1 (invertible ideal; the frozen working definition).**
> Let $R$ be an integral domain. An ideal $I \subseteq R$ is **invertible** if there exist an
> ideal $J \subseteq R$ and an element $x \in R$ with $x \neq 0$ such that
> $$I J = xR.$$
> For $R = \mathcal{O}_d$ this is the Lean predicate `QuadraticOrder.IsInvertibleIdeal`.

> **Proposition 2 (arithmetic of invertible ideals).**
> Let $R$ be an integral domain with fraction field $F$, and let $I, J, A, B \subseteq R$ be
> ideals.
> 1. **(a)** If $I$ is invertible then $I \neq 0$.
> 2. **(b)** Every nonzero principal ideal is invertible; in particular $R$ itself is
>    invertible.
> 3. **(c)** If $I$ and $J$ are invertible, so is $IJ$.
> 4. **(d)** If $IJ$ is invertible, so are $I$ and $J$.
> 5. **(e)** Invertible ideals cancel: if $I$ is invertible and $IA = IB$, then $A = B$.
> 6. **(f)** If $I$ is invertible then $I$ is finitely generated.
> 7. **(g)** If $R$ is local and $I$ is invertible, then $I$ is principal, generated by an
>    element of $I$.

> **Theorem 3 (invertible $\iff$ locally principal).**
> Let $R$ be an integral domain and $I \subseteq R$ a nonzero finitely generated ideal. The
> following are equivalent:
> 1. **(i)** $I$ is invertible;
> 2. **(ii)** $I R_\mathfrak{p}$ is a principal ideal of $R_\mathfrak{p}$ for every prime ideal
>    $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$;
> 3. **(iii)** $I R_\mathfrak{m}$ is a principal ideal of $R_\mathfrak{m}$ for every maximal
>    ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $R$;
> 4. **(iv)** $I R_\mathfrak{m}$ is a principal ideal of $R_\mathfrak{m}$ for every maximal
>    ideal $\mathfrak{m} \supseteq I$;
> 5. **(v)** $I R_\mathfrak{m}$ is an invertible ideal of $R_\mathfrak{m}$ for every maximal
>    ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $R$.
>
> The implications (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii), (iii), (iv), (v) hold without the finite-generation
> hypothesis. Moreover, when these hold, then with
> $I^{-1} := (R :_F I) = \{\beta \in F : \beta I \subseteq R\}$ one has $I\, I^{-1} = R$, and
> for **every** nonzero $a \in I$ the ideal $J := a I^{-1} \subseteq R$ satisfies $IJ = aR$.
>
> In particular ((i) $\iff$ (v)): *invertibility is a local property.* Since $\mathcal{O}_d$ is
> a Noetherian domain, the equivalence applies to every nonzero ideal
> $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d$ with no finiteness hypothesis.

> **Lemma 4 (triviality away from the index).**
> Let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d$ be an ideal of finite index
> $n = [\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}]$, and let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}$
> of residue characteristic $q$ with $q \nmid n$. Then $\mathfrak{a} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}$
> and
> $$\mathfrak{a}\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m} = \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m} \quad \text{(the unit ideal).}$$
> In particular:
> 1. **(a)** if $[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = p^m$ for a rational prime $p$ and $\mathfrak{q}$
>    is any maximal ideal lying over a rational prime $q \neq p$, then
>    $\mathfrak{a}\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{q} = \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{q}$;
> 2. **(b)** every maximal ideal containing $\mathfrak{a}$ has residue characteristic dividing
>    $n$; for $n = p^m$ with $m \ge 1$, every maximal ideal containing $\mathfrak{a}$ lies over
>    $p$.

> **Corollary 5 (invertibility of a $p\text{-power-index}$ ideal is a condition at $p$ alone).**
> Let $p$ be a rational prime and $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d$ an ideal of index $p^m$
> ($m \ge 0$). Then $\mathfrak{a}$ is invertible if and only if
> $\mathfrak{a}\,\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is principal for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$
> of $\mathcal{O}$ lying over $p$.

> **Theorem 6 (index prime to the conductor $\Rightarrow$ invertible).**
> Let $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d$ be an ideal of finite index
> $n = [\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}]$ with $\gcd(n, f) = 1$. Then $\mathfrak{a}$ is invertible.

(The auxiliary Lemmas 7 and 8 — the conductor ideal $fO_K$ and the DVR property of
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ away from the conductor — are stated in the course of the proof of
Theorem 6.)

## Role in the development

The thesis states Corollary 3.1.3 and the Lauter–Viray count $A(N)$ for *invertible* ideals but
proves the corollary by counting *locally principal* ideals (§3.3 defines
$R(p^m) := \#\{\mathfrak a \subseteq \mathcal O : \mathfrak a \text{ locally principal},\ [\mathcal O : \mathfrak a] = p^m\}$),
and its notation section (§1.2) identifies the two notions in passing without proof. This
document is the missing bridge: it fixes the definition of invertibility frozen in Lean and
proves the interface theorems that Corollary 3.1.3\* (doc *cor-3-1-3*, WP‑G), the $A(N)$
definition (WP‑I), and the CRT gluing of counts (WP‑H) consume. It uses only the order and its
$\{1,\tau\}\text{-basis}$ (doc *notation*) and the finite-index layer (doc *infra-index*);
Corollary 5 is what reduces invertibility of the normal-form ideals $(p^k,\, p^{m-k}(\tau - A))$
to the local analysis of Lemmas 3.2.7/3.2.8 at the unique prime over $p$.

## Proof

### Preliminaries

**Facts imported from the index layer** (doc *infra-index*; one-line arguments recalled for
self-containment).

- **(I0)** $\mathcal{O}_d$ is a Noetherian integral domain. *(Domain:
  $\mathcal{O}_d \subseteq K$. Noetherian: $\mathcal{O}_d \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ as a
  $\mathbb{Z}\text{-module}$ and $\mathbb{Z}$ is Noetherian, so every ideal is finitely
  generated already as a $\mathbb{Z}\text{-module}$.)*
- **(I1)** Every nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$ has finite index. *(Pick
  $0 \neq \alpha \in \mathfrak{a}$; then
  $N(\alpha) = \alpha\bar\alpha \in \mathfrak{a} \cap \mathbb{Z}$ is nonzero since $\mathcal O$
  is a domain and conjugation is injective, and
  $\mathcal{O}/N(\alpha)\mathcal{O} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/N(\alpha))^2$ is finite.)* Conversely an
  ideal of finite index is nonzero, since $\mathcal{O}$ is infinite.
- **(I2)** (Lagrange) If $n = [\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] < \infty$ then
  $n\mathcal{O} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}$. *(The exponent of the finite abelian group
  $\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a}$ divides its order.)*
- **(I3)** Every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $\mathcal{O}$ has a residue characteristic:
  $\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z} = q\mathbb{Z}$ for a unique rational prime $q$. *(No rational
  prime is a unit of $\mathcal{O}$ — a unit has norm $\pm 1$, while $N(\ell) = \ell^2$ — so
  $\mathfrak{m} \neq 0$; hence $\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z}$ contains some $N(\alpha) \neq 0$
  by the argument of (I1), and it is a proper prime ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ because
  $1 \notin \mathfrak{m}$.)*

**Localization conventions** (standard; e.g. Atiyah–Macdonald, Ch. 3). $R$ an integral domain
with fraction field $F$, $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq R$ prime, $R_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq F$ as
above, $I \subseteq R$ an ideal.

- **(L1)** Every element of $I R_\mathfrak{p}$ has the form $a/s$ with $a \in I$,
  $s \notin \mathfrak{p}$. *(Clear denominators in a finite sum $\sum_j i_j r_j / s_j$: the
  common denominator is $\prod_j s_j \notin \mathfrak{p}$, and the numerator lands in $I$
  because $I$ is an ideal.)*
- **(L2)** $\mathfrak{p} R_\mathfrak{p} \cap R = \mathfrak{p}$. *($\supseteq$ is clear.
  $\subseteq$: if $\alpha \in R$ and $\alpha = a/s$ with $a \in \mathfrak{p}$,
  $s \notin \mathfrak{p}$ (using (L1)), then $s\alpha = a \in \mathfrak{p}$ in the domain $R$,
  and $\mathfrak{p}$ prime with $s \notin \mathfrak{p}$ forces $\alpha \in \mathfrak{p}$.)*
- **(L3)** $R_\mathfrak{p}$ is a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}R_\mathfrak{p}$.
  *(Properness: $1 \in \mathfrak{p}R_\mathfrak{p}$ would give $s = a \in \mathfrak{p}$ by the
  computation in (L2), a contradiction. If $a/s \notin \mathfrak{p}R_\mathfrak{p}$ then
  $a \notin \mathfrak{p}$, so $s/a \in R_\mathfrak{p}$ is an inverse; a ring in which every
  element outside one proper ideal is a unit is local.)*
- **(L4)** Extension of ideals is multiplicative:
  $(IJ)R_\mathfrak{p} = (I R_\mathfrak{p})(J R_\mathfrak{p})$. *(Both sides are generated over
  $R_\mathfrak{p}$ by the products $ij$, $i \in I$, $j \in J$.)*
- **(L5)** If $I \neq 0$ then $I R_\mathfrak{p} \neq 0$. *(In a domain, $R \to R_\mathfrak{p}$
  is injective and $I \subseteq I R_\mathfrak{p}$.)*

### Proof of Proposition 2

**(a)** If $I = 0$ then $IJ = 0 \neq xR$ for every $x \neq 0$.

**(b)** $(aR) \cdot R = aR$ with $a \neq 0$, so take $J = R$, $x = a$.

**(c)** Say $I J_1 = xR$ and $J J_2 = yR$ with $x, y \neq 0$. Then
$(IJ)(J_1 J_2) = (I J_1)(J J_2) = (xR)(yR) = xyR$, and $xy \neq 0$ in the domain $R$.

**(d)** Say $(IJ)L = xR$, $x \neq 0$. Then $I(JL) = xR$ and $J(IL) = xR$, so both $I$ and $J$
are invertible.

**(e)** Let $IJ = xR$, $x \neq 0$, and suppose $IA = IB$. Multiplying by $J$:
$xA = (IJ)A = (IJ)B = xB$. For $a \in A$: $xa \in xB$, so $xa = xb$ for some $b \in B$, and
cancelling $x$ in the domain gives $a = b \in B$. Thus $A \subseteq B$; symmetrically
$B \subseteq A$.

**Common device for (f) and (g).** Let $IJ = xR$, $x \neq 0$. Since $x \in IJ$, write
$$x = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i c_i, \qquad a_i \in I, \; c_i \in J,$$
and set $b_i := c_i / x \in F$. Then
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i = 1, \qquad \text{and} \qquad b_i I \;=\; \tfrac{1}{x}\, c_i I \;\subseteq\; \tfrac{1}{x}\, J I \;=\; \tfrac{1}{x}\, xR \;=\; R \quad (1 \le i \le n). \tag{$\ast$}$$
In particular $a b_i \in R$ for every $a \in I$.

**(f)** For $a \in I$: $a = a \cdot 1 = \sum_i a_i (a b_i)$ with each $a b_i \in R$ by ($\ast$),
so $I = (a_1, \dots, a_n)$.

**(g)** Let $R$ be local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$. Each $a_i b_i$ lies in $R$ by
($\ast$). If all $a_i b_i \in \mathfrak{m}$, then $1 = \sum_i a_i b_i \in \mathfrak{m}$, absurd;
so $u := a_{i_0} b_{i_0} \notin \mathfrak{m}$ for some $i_0$, and $u$ is a unit of the local
ring $R$. For every $a \in I$:
$$a \;=\; u^{-1} u\, a \;=\; a_{i_0} \cdot \big(u^{-1}\,(b_{i_0} a)\big) \in a_{i_0} R,$$
since $b_{i_0} a \in R$ by ($\ast$). Hence $I = a_{i_0}R$ with $a_{i_0} \in I$. $\blacksquare$

### Proof of Theorem 3

**(i) $\Rightarrow$ (v)** (no finiteness needed). Let $IJ = xR$, $x \neq 0$, and let
$\mathfrak{m}$ be maximal. By (L4),
$$(I R_\mathfrak{m})(J R_\mathfrak{m}) = (IJ) R_\mathfrak{m} = x R_\mathfrak{m},$$
and $x \neq 0$ in $R_\mathfrak{m}$; so $I R_\mathfrak{m}$ is invertible in $R_\mathfrak{m}$
(whose fraction field is again $F$).

**(v) $\Rightarrow$ (iii)** (no finiteness needed). $R_\mathfrak{m}$ is a local domain by (L3),
so Proposition 2(g) applies: $I R_\mathfrak{m}$ is principal.

**(i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii)** (no finiteness needed). The argument for (i) $\Rightarrow$ (v) works
verbatim at any prime $\mathfrak{p}$ (only (L3), (L4) were used), and Proposition 2(g) applies
to the local domain $R_\mathfrak{p}$.

**(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii) and (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (iv)**: immediate specializations (maximal
ideals are prime).

**(iv) $\Rightarrow$ (iii).** Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be maximal with $I \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}$.
Pick $s \in I \smallsetminus \mathfrak{m}$; then $s$ is a unit of $R_\mathfrak{m}$ by (L3) and
$s \in I R_\mathfrak{m}$, so $I R_\mathfrak{m} = R_\mathfrak{m} = 1 \cdot R_\mathfrak{m}$ is
principal.

**(iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i)** (here $I \neq 0$ finitely generated is used). Write
$I = (x_1, \dots, x_r)$ and set
$$I^{-1} := (R :_F I) = \{\beta \in F : \beta I \subseteq R\}.$$
Then $I\,I^{-1}$ — the set of finite sums $\sum_k \alpha_k \beta_k$ with $\alpha_k \in I$,
$\beta_k \in I^{-1}$ — is contained in $R$ by definition of $I^{-1}$ and is an ideal of $R$.

*Claim: $I\,I^{-1} = R$.* Suppose not; then $I\,I^{-1} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ for some maximal
ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ (every proper ideal lies in one). By (iii),
$I R_\mathfrak{m} = \gamma R_\mathfrak{m}$ for some $\gamma \in I R_\mathfrak{m}$. By (L1),
$\gamma = a/s$ with $a \in I$, $s \notin \mathfrak{m}$; since $1/s$ is a unit of
$R_\mathfrak{m}$,
$$I R_\mathfrak{m} = a R_\mathfrak{m}, \qquad a \in I .$$
Here $a \neq 0$: otherwise $I R_\mathfrak{m} = 0$, contradicting (L5). Now for each generator
$x_j \in I \subseteq I R_\mathfrak{m}$ write $x_j = a\, r_j / s_j$ with $r_j \in R$,
$s_j \notin \mathfrak{m}$, i.e. $s_j x_j \in aR$. Put $s := s_1 s_2 \cdots s_r$; then
$s \notin \mathfrak{m}$ ($\mathfrak{m}$ is prime) and $s x_j \in aR$ for every $j$, hence
$sI \subseteq aR$, i.e.
$$\frac{s}{a}\, I \subseteq R, \qquad \text{so} \qquad \frac{s}{a} \in I^{-1}.$$
But then $s = a \cdot \tfrac{s}{a} \in I\, I^{-1} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$, contradicting
$s \notin \mathfrak{m}$. This proves the claim.

Finally, pick any $a \in I$ with $a \neq 0$ (possible as $I \neq 0$) and set $J := a\, I^{-1}$.
Then $J \subseteq I\,I^{-1} = R$ (using $a \in I$), so $J$ is an (integral) ideal of $R$, and
$$I J = a\,(I\, I^{-1}) = aR, \qquad a \neq 0.$$
Thus $I$ is invertible, and the "moreover" clause holds: the element $a$ was an arbitrary
nonzero element of $I$. (For the "moreover" clause under hypothesis (i) rather than (iii): (i)
implies (iii) by the chain above without finiteness, and (i) implies finite generation by
Proposition 2(f), so the argument just given applies.) $\blacksquare$

**Remark on the cycle structure.** The implications proved are (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii)
$\Rightarrow$ (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (iv) $\Rightarrow$ (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) and (i)
$\Rightarrow$ (v) $\Rightarrow$ (iii), which closes all equivalences. Only (iii) $\Rightarrow$
(i) uses finite generation; by (I0) it is automatic for $R = \mathcal{O}_d$.

### Proof of Lemma 4

Let $n = [\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}]$ and let $\mathfrak{m}$ have residue characteristic
$q \nmid n$. By (I2), $n \in n\mathcal{O} \subseteq \mathfrak{a}$. If $n \in \mathfrak{m}$, then
$n \in \mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z} = q\mathbb{Z}$, i.e. $q \mid n$ — contradiction. Hence
$n \in \mathfrak{a} \smallsetminus \mathfrak{m}$; in particular
$\mathfrak{a} \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}$, and $n$ is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ by
(L3) lying in $\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$, so
$\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m} = \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$.

**(a)** If $n = p^m$ and $\mathfrak{q}$ lies over $q \neq p$, then $q \nmid p^m$, and the above
applies. **(b)** is the contrapositive of the first assertion: if
$\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$ then $q \mid n$; and when $n = p^m$ with $m \ge 1$,
$q \mid p^m$ forces $q = p$. $\blacksquare$

### Proof of Corollary 5

The index $p^m$ is finite, so $\mathfrak{a} \neq 0$ by (I1), and $\mathcal{O}_d$ is Noetherian
by (I0); Theorem 3 applies.

($\Rightarrow$) If $\mathfrak{a}$ is invertible, then by Theorem 3 (i) $\Rightarrow$ (iii),
$\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ is principal at *every* maximal ideal, in particular at
those over $p$.

($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is principal for every maximal
$\mathfrak{p}$ over $p$. We verify Theorem 3 (iv): let $\mathfrak{m} \supseteq \mathfrak{a}$ be
maximal. If $m = 0$ then $\mathfrak{a} = \mathcal{O}$ (index 1) and no such $\mathfrak{m}$
exists, so (iv) holds vacuously. If $m \ge 1$, Lemma 4(b) shows $\mathfrak{m}$ lies over $p$, so
$\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ is principal by hypothesis. By Theorem 3 (iv)
$\Rightarrow$ (i), $\mathfrak{a}$ is invertible. $\blacksquare$

### The conductor ideal and the local structure away from it

> **Lemma 7 (the conductor ideal).**
> $f O_K \subseteq \mathcal{O}_d$, and $f O_K$ is simultaneously an ideal of $O_K$ and of
> $\mathcal{O}_d$. Moreover $\mathcal{O}_d = \mathbb{Z} + f O_K$.

**Proof.** First, $\tau = f\omega + \tfrac{Df(f-1)}{2}$: indeed
$$\tau - f\omega = \frac{Df^2 + f\sqrt D}{2} - \frac{fD + f\sqrt D}{2} = \frac{Df(f-1)}{2},$$
which is an integer because $f(f-1)$ is even. Hence
$f\omega = \tau - \tfrac{Df(f-1)}{2} \in \mathcal{O}$, and
$$f O_K = f\mathbb{Z} + f\omega\,\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Z} + \tau\mathbb{Z} = \mathcal{O}.$$
(Also $\tau = f\omega + \text{integer} \in O_K$, so $\mathcal{O} \subseteq O_K$.) The set $fO_K$
is an $O_K\text{-ideal}$; being closed under multiplication by all of
$O_K \supseteq \mathcal{O}$, it is in particular an $\mathcal{O}\text{-ideal}$. Finally
$\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z} + \tau\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Z} + fO_K$ (as
$\tau \in \mathbb{Z} + f\omega\mathbb{Z}$), and $\mathbb{Z} + fO_K \subseteq \mathcal{O}$; so
$\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z} + fO_K$. $\blacksquare$

(Cf. Cox, *Primes of the Form* $x^2+ny^2$, Lemma 7.2 and the discussion of the conductor in §7;
$fO_K$ is contained in the conductor ideal $\mathfrak{f} = (\mathcal{O} :_K O_K)$, and in fact
equals it, but only the containment $fO_K \subseteq \mathcal{O}$ is needed here.)

> **Lemma 8 (DVR away from the conductor).**
> Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_d$ of residue characteristic $q$ with
> $q \nmid f$. Then, inside $K$:
> 1. **(1)** $O_K \subseteq \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$;
> 2. **(2)** $\mathfrak{M} := \mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m} \cap O_K$ is a nonzero prime
>    ideal of $O_K$ with $\mathfrak{M} \cap \mathcal{O} = \mathfrak{m}$;
> 3. **(3)** $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m} = (O_K)_\mathfrak{M}$;
> 4. **(4)** $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ is a discrete valuation ring. In particular, for every
>    nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}$, the ideal
>    $\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ is principal.

**Proof.**
**(1)** $f \notin \mathfrak{m}$: otherwise $f \in \mathfrak{m} \cap \mathbb{Z} = q\mathbb{Z}$,
i.e. $q \mid f$. So $f$ is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ by (L3). For $\beta \in O_K$ we
have $f\beta \in fO_K \subseteq \mathcal{O}$ by Lemma 7, hence
$\beta = (f\beta)/f \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$.

**(2)** $\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ is the maximal ideal of the local ring
$\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ (L3); its contraction $\mathfrak{M}$ to the subring $O_K$ (legitimate
by (1)) is a prime ideal of $O_K$. Its further contraction to $\mathcal{O}$ is
$\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m} \cap \mathcal{O} = \mathfrak{m}$ by (L2). It is nonzero:
$q \in \mathfrak{m} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ and $q \in O_K$, so
$q \in \mathfrak{M}$.

**(3)** ($\subseteq$) An element of $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ is $\alpha/s$ with
$\alpha \in \mathcal{O} \subseteq O_K$ and $s \in \mathcal{O} \smallsetminus \mathfrak{m}$. If
$s$ were in $\mathfrak{M}$, then $s \in \mathfrak{M} \cap \mathcal{O} = \mathfrak{m}$ by (2), a
contradiction; so $s \in O_K \smallsetminus \mathfrak{M}$ and $\alpha/s \in (O_K)_\mathfrak{M}$.
($\supseteq$) An element of $(O_K)_\mathfrak{M}$ is $\beta/t$ with $\beta \in O_K$,
$t \in O_K \smallsetminus \mathfrak{M}$. By (1), $\beta, t \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$; and
$t \notin \mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ (by definition of $\mathfrak{M}$ as the
contraction), so $t$ is a unit of the local ring $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ and
$\beta/t = \beta\, t^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$.

**(4)** $O_K$ is a Dedekind domain and $\mathfrak{M}$ is a nonzero prime, so the localization
$(O_K)_\mathfrak{M}$ is a discrete valuation ring (standard: e.g. Neukirch, *Algebraic Number
Theory*, Ch. I §11; Atiyah–Macdonald, Thm 9.3). Every nonzero ideal of a DVR is a power of the
maximal ideal, hence principal; and $\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m} \neq 0$ by (L5).
$\blacksquare$

### Proof of Theorem 6

Let $n = [\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] < \infty$ with $\gcd(n, f) = 1$; then
$\mathfrak{a} \neq 0$ by (I1), and $\mathcal{O}_d$ is Noetherian by (I0). We verify condition
(iv) of Theorem 3. Let $\mathfrak{m} \supseteq \mathfrak{a}$ be a maximal ideal, of residue
characteristic $q$ (I3). By Lemma 4(b), $q \mid n$; since $\gcd(n, f) = 1$, this forces
$q \nmid f$. By Lemma 8(4), $\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{m}$ is principal. As
$\mathfrak{m}$ was arbitrary, Theorem 3 (iv) $\Rightarrow$ (i) shows $\mathfrak{a}$ is
invertible.

$\blacksquare$

## Remarks

**Remark 1 (relation to the thesis; why this document exists).** This result does not appear in
the thesis; it is used silently in two places. (1) §1.2 (Notation, p. 2) defines the class group
as "the group of locally principal (invertible) ideals modulo principal ideals" — the
parenthetical *is* Theorem 3, asserted without proof. (2) Corollary 3.1.3 is stated for
*invertible* ideals, but its proof in §3.3 (p. 22) counts the ideals $\mathfrak{a}$ with
$[\mathcal{O} : \mathfrak{a}] = p^m$ that are *locally principal* (the quantity $R(p^m)$ defined
there); no bridge between the two notions is ever stated. Theorem 3 together with Corollary 5 is
that bridge, and additionally justifies the thesis's implicit restriction of the local analysis
to the primes over $p$ (Lemma 4). Relatedly, ERRATA E6.1 notes that the display of Corollary
3.1.3 typographically coincides with that of Theorem 3.1.2 while meaning the invertible count;
the corrected statement (ERRATA E5) is quantified over invertible ideals in the sense of
Definition 1. Nothing in ERRATA affects the *content* of the present document.

**Remark 2 (comparison with the usual definitions; why this definition was frozen).** The
textbook definition (Neukirch I §12; Cox §7) calls a fractional $\mathcal{O}\text{-ideal}$
$\mathfrak{b} \subseteq K$ invertible when $\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b} = \mathcal{O}$ for some
fractional ideal $\mathfrak{a}$. For a nonzero integral ideal $I$ the two notions agree: if
$IJ = x\mathcal{O}$ per Definition 1 then $I \cdot (x^{-1}J) = \mathcal{O}$; conversely if
$I\mathfrak{b} = \mathcal{O}$, pick $0 \neq a \in I$ and set
$J := a\mathfrak{b} \subseteq I\mathfrak{b} = \mathcal{O}$, an integral ideal with
$IJ = a\mathcal{O}$. The inverse is unique and equals $I^{-1} = (\mathcal{O} :_K I)$: if
$I\mathfrak{b} = \mathcal{O}$ then
$\mathfrak{b} \subseteq I^{-1} = I^{-1}(I\mathfrak{b}) = (I^{-1}I)\mathfrak{b} \subseteq \mathfrak{b}$.
Definition 1 was frozen for Lean because it mentions only integral ideals and one ring element —
no fractional-ideal API is needed. Mathlib's `FractionalIdeal` invertibility theory is
essentially Dedekind-gated, and $\mathcal{O}_d$ is not Dedekind for $f > 1$; the audit and any
gap-filling are scheduled with WP‑G. A second classical characterization — $I$ invertible iff
$I$ is *proper*, i.e. $\{\beta \in K : \beta I \subseteq I\} = \mathcal{O}$ — is special to
quadratic orders (Cox, Prop. 7.4) and fails in general rings; the locally-principal
characterization of Theorem 3 is the robust one, and it is the one the development builds on.

**Remark 3 (classical form of the coprimality hypothesis).** Cox (§7, Lemma 7.18) says an
$\mathcal{O}\text{-ideal}$ $\mathfrak{a}$ is *prime to the conductor* when
$\mathfrak{a} + f\mathcal{O} = \mathcal{O}$, and shows this is equivalent to
$\gcd(N(\mathfrak{a}), f) = 1$. With index in place of norm the equivalence is elementary: if
$\gcd(n, f) = 1$ (where $n = [\mathcal{O}:\mathfrak{a}]$), write $1 = an + bf$ with
$a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$; then $1 \in \mathfrak{a} + f\mathcal{O}$ by (I2). Conversely, if some
prime $q$ divides $\gcd(n, f)$, then $\mathfrak{a} + q\mathcal{O}$ is proper — otherwise
$1 = \alpha + q\beta$ ($\alpha \in \mathfrak{a}$, $\beta \in \mathcal{O}$) makes multiplication
by $q$ surjective on the finite group $M = \mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a}$, hence bijective, forcing
$M[q] = 0$ and (by Cauchy's theorem) $q \nmid n = \#M$ — and then
$\mathfrak{a} + f\mathcal{O} \subseteq \mathfrak{a} + q\mathcal{O} \subsetneq \mathcal{O}$. So
Theorem 6 is exactly the classical "ideals prime to the conductor are invertible", with the
hypothesis phrased through the index because the index is the norm used throughout the
development.

**Remark 4 (sharpness).** The converse of Theorem 6 fails, and the failure is the subject of
Corollary 3.1.3\*: for $p \mid f$ the principal ideal $p\mathcal{O}$ has index $p^2$ (not prime
to $f$) and is invertible by Proposition 2(b). The content of Corollary 3.1.3\* (doc
*cor-3-1-3*) is precisely the count of invertible ideals of index $p^m$ for $p \mid f$, i.e. of
the invertible ideals *not* covered by Theorem 6; by Corollary 5 that count reduces to a local
computation at the unique prime of $\mathcal{O}$ over $p$ (uniqueness for $p \mid f$ is proved
in the prime-classification documents, cf. Prop 3.2.1/Rem 3.2.3, and is not needed in this
document).

**Remark 5 (formalization pitfalls).**
1. *Maximal vs. prime localization.* Theorem 3 deliberately includes both quantifications, (ii)
   and (iii)–(iv): the cheap direction (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) holds at all primes, while the
   substantive converse only needs maximal ideals containing $I$. The frozen Lean statement
   quantifies over *all* primes (`IsLocallyPrincipal` is form (ii)); the equivalence with the
   weakest condition (iv) is what makes it verifiable — a proof only ever checks the maximal
   ideals containing $I$ (Corollary 5).
2. *The zero ideal and the unit ideal.* Definition 1 carries no nonzeroness or finiteness
   hypotheses; $I \neq 0$ and $I$ f.g. are consequences (Proposition 2(a), (f)). $\mathcal{O}$
   itself is invertible, and Corollary 5 at $m = 0$ is the (true, vacuous-feeling) statement
   that $\mathcal{O}$ is invertible iff it is principal at the primes over $p$.
3. *Lean's `QuadraticOrder d` is defined for all $d$.* The codebase defines the ring for every
   integer $d$; the domain instance (needed by every statement here) holds when $d$ is not a
   perfect square, in particular for all $d < 0$. The interface lemmas must carry the
   discriminant hypotheses (or the `IsDomain` instance) explicitly.
4. *No index multiplicativity.* Nothing in this document uses multiplicativity of the ideal
   index — which is false for non-invertible ideals of $\mathcal{O}_d$ and is Dedekind-gated in
   Mathlib (`Ideal.absNorm`). Only (I1)–(I3) from the index layer are consumed.
5. *Lemma 8 genuinely needs $q \nmid f$.* At a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ over $p \mid f$ the local
   ring $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ is *not* a DVR (WP‑D's guardrail lemma); there exist
   non-invertible ideals of $p\text{-power}$ index, which is why Theorem 3.1.2\* and Corollary
   3.1.3\* differ. No step of Lemma 8 survives without the coprimality hypothesis: for
   $q \mid f$, $O_K \not\subseteq \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$.
6. *Localization API.* The intended Lean phrasing localizes at `Localization.AtPrime` and
   expresses principality via `Submodule.IsPrincipal` of the mapped ideal; Lemma 8 should be
   built on the conductor machinery already in Mathlib
   (`Localization.localRingHom_bijective_of_not_conductor_le` is the germ of statement (3)).
