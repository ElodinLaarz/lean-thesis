# Corollary 3.1.3* (CORRECTED): invertible ideals of norm $p^m$

## Statement

> Let $D<0$ be a fundamental discriminant, let $f\geq1$, put $d=Df^2$, and let
> $p\mid f$ be prime.  Write $w=v_p(f)$ and let
> $v=v_p(d)$ for odd $p$, while $v=v_2(d)-2=v_2(d/4)$ for $p=2$.
> Let $C^{\times}_d(p^m)$ be the number of invertible integral ideals of
> $\mathcal O_d=\mathbb Z[\tau]$, $\tau=(d+\sqrt d)/2$, having index $p^m$.
>
> In the **trivial regime**
> $$m<v\quad\text{or}\quad(m=v\text{ and }v=2w-2),$$
> one has
> $$C^{\times}_d(p^m)= \begin{cases}p^{m/2},&m\text{ even},\\0,&m\text{ odd}. \end{cases}$$
> In the complementary **case regime**
> $$v<m\quad\text{or}\quad(m=v\text{ and }2w\leq v),$$
> one has
> $$C^{\times}_d(p^m)= \begin{cases} p^w,&p\text{ ramifies in }\mathbb Q(\sqrt D),\\ p^w+p^{w-1},&p\text{ is inert and }m\text{ is even},\\ 0,&p\text{ is inert and }m\text{ is odd},\\ (p^w-p^{w-1})(m+1-2w),&p\text{ splits}. \end{cases}$$
>
> Equivalently, the three cases are selected by the Kronecker symbol
> $(D/p)=0,-1,1$.  The boundary $m=v=2w-2$ occurs precisely for $p=2$ and
> odd $D$ and belongs to the trivial regime.  Thus $m=0$ gives one ideal.

## Role in the development

This is the invertible refinement of corrected Theorem 3.1.2*.  It supplies
the local factor used in the Chapter 2 quantity $A(N)$.  Its proof uses the
normal-form bijection of Lemma 3.2.4, the unique prime over a conductor prime,
and the contraction/equality criteria of Lemmas 3.2.7 and 3.2.8.  The unusual
boundary is forced by the $2\text{-adic}$ calculation; moving it to the case regime
produces the negative value in ERRATA E5.

## Proof

**Step 1 (normal form and the invertibility criterion).** Put
$$g(X)=X^2-dX+\frac{d^2-d}{4},$$
the minimal polynomial of $\tau$.  We use the following normal-form fact,
including its uniqueness assertion.  Every ideal of index $p^m$ has a unique
presentation
$$ \mathfrak a_{k,A}=p^k\mathbb Z\oplus p^{m-k}(\tau-A)\mathbb Z, \qquad \lceil m/2\rceil\leq k\leq m, \tag{1} $$
where $A$ is a residue class modulo $p^s$, $s=2k-m$, and
$g(A)\equiv0\pmod {p^s}$.  The prime over $p$ is unique, so an ideal in (1)
is invertible exactly when its localization there is principal.

Set $r=m-k$, so $s=m-2r$.  Lemma 3.2.7 says that the contraction of the local
principal ideal generated by $p^r(\tau-A)$ has normal-form parameters
$$k'=r+\nu,\qquad m'=2r+\nu, \quad \nu=v_p(g(A)).\tag{2}$$
For $s>0$, uniqueness in (1), together with Lemma 3.2.8, therefore gives
$$\mathfrak a_{k,A}\text{ invertible}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_p(g(A))=s.\tag{3}$$
The condition is independent of the representative of $A$: modulo $p$ the
polynomial $g$ has a repeated root at a conductor prime, hence
$p\mid g'(A)$ whenever $p\mid g(A)$; consequently
$$g(A+p^st)-g(A)=p^stg'(A)+p^{2s}t^2\equiv0\pmod {p^{s+1}}.$$
When $s=0$ (which occurs only for even $m$), (1) is the principal ideal
$p^{m/2}\mathcal O_d$, and contributes one ideal.

**Step 2 (reduction to an exact-valuation count).** Let $E_s$ be the number of
classes $A\bmod p^s$ satisfying the exact condition in (3), and put $E_0=1$.
The invertible count is now
$$C^{\times}_d(p^m)= \sum_{\substack{0\leq s\leq m\\s\equiv m\pmod2}}E_s.\tag{4}$$
It remains to evaluate this finite sum.

**Step 3 (completing the square).**  For odd $p$, translation by $d/2$ and multiplication by
the square unit $4^{-1}$ reduce $g(A)$ to $x^2-q$, where
$q=d/4$ in $\mathbb Z_p$ and $v_p(q)=v$.  For $p=2$, the hypothesis $2\mid f$
gives $4\mid d$, so the same integral translation gives $q=d/4$ and
$v_2(q)=v$.  Write $q=p^vu$ with $u$ a $p\text{-adic}$ unit.

**Step 4 (the sub-boundary block).** First suppose $0<s<v$.  If
$v_p(x^2-q)=s$, then $v_p(x^2)=s$; hence $s$ is
even, say $s=2t$.  The admissible classes are
$x=p^ty$ with $y$ a unit modulo $p^t$, so
$$E_{2t}=p^t-p^{t-1},\qquad E_{2t+1}=0.\tag{5}$$
Together with $E_0=1$, the even terms telescope:
$$1+\sum_{t=1}^{h}(p^t-p^{t-1})=p^h.\tag{6}$$
Equations (4)–(6) prove the trivial formula when $m<v$.

**Step 5 (boundary and tail).** We next record the boundary and tail
calculation.  It follows by writing
$x=p^{\lfloor v/2\rfloor}y$ and reducing $y^2-u$ modulo $p$, modulo $4$, or
modulo $8$, as appropriate.

* If $v=2w+1$ (the odd-valuation ramified cases), the only boundary
  contribution has size $p^w$, and no class can occur above the boundary:
  a square cannot have the odd valuation $v$.
* If $v=2w$ and $p$ is odd, the sum through the boundary is respectively
  $p^w+p^{w-1}$ in the inert case and $p^w-p^{w-1}$ in the split case.
  Above the boundary there are no inert roots, while each permitted exponent
  contributes $2(p^w-p^{w-1})$ in the split case (one branch around each of
  the two simple roots of $u$ modulo $p$).
* If $v=2w$ and $p=2$ with $D/4\equiv3\pmod4$, the sum through the even
  boundary is $2^w$; at the odd exponent $v+1$ there are $2^w$ exact classes,
  and there are none thereafter because $u\equiv3\pmod4$ is not a square.
* If $p=2$, $D$ is odd, and $v=2w-2$, the even sum through $v$ is
  $2^{w-1}$.  This proves the stated trivial boundary.  Above it, if
  $u\equiv5\pmod8$ (the inert case), exponent $v+2$ contributes $2^w$ and all
  other tail exponents contribute zero.  If $u\equiv1\pmod8$ (the split
  case), exponents $v+1,v+2$ contribute zero and every permitted exponent
  at least $v+3$ contributes $2^w=2(2^w-2^{w-1})$.

For completeness, these numbers come from the following exhaustive residue
tests.  For odd $p$, a nonsquare $u$ has no root modulo $p$, while a square
has two roots and Hensel lifting preserves the two branches; requiring exact
rather than higher valuation removes one factor-$p$ subball from each branch.
For $p=2$, every odd square is $1$ modulo $8$: if $u\equiv3\pmod4$ the
difference of two odd terms has valuation exactly one; if $u\equiv5\pmod8$
it has valuation exactly two; and if $u\equiv1\pmod8$ the four square-root
branches start at modulus $8$.  Even $y$ gives an odd difference.  Counting
the free higher digits gives exactly the powers displayed above.

**Step 6 (assembly).** Summing the list in (4) finishes the proof.  In the ramified cases the total
is $p^w$.  Inert tails vanish except for the $2\text{-adic}$ $v+2$ boundary, giving
$p^w+p^{w-1}$ for even $m$ and zero for odd $m$.  In the split case the
initial contribution is $p^w-p^{w-1}$ and each two-step increase of $m$ adds
$2(p^w-p^{w-1})$; hence the total is
$$(p^w-p^{w-1})(m+1-2w).$$
This is the asserted case formula and covers every value of $m$.

$\blacksquare$

## Remarks

**Remark 1 (Divergence from the thesis — ERRATA E5: the regime boundary).**
The thesis assigns every equality $m=v$ to the case regime.  That is correct
when $v\geq2w$, but false when $p=2$ and $D$ is odd, for then $v=2w-2$ and the
equality belongs to the telescoping block (6).  For $d=-112$, $w=2$, $m=v=2$,
the corrected answer is $2$; the misplaced split formula gives a negative
integer.  The regression harness also pins $d=-48$ and $d=-192$ at this
boundary.
